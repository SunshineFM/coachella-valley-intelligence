## 2026-02-10: Signal Drop

**Anthropic's Daniéla Amodei Says Hire for Heart, Not Hard Skills**
Anthropic's co-founder told ABC News the company prioritizes emotional intelligence, kindness, and curiosity over purely technical backgrounds when hiring — a direct challenge to two decades of STEM-first workforce dogma. For Coachella Valley employers wrestling with what skills matter in an AI-saturated world, this is permission to stop chasing credentials and start looking for humans who can actually connect. The question isn't whether your team can prompt an LLM. It's whether they can build trust with a customer who's scared, confused, or angry.

**Spotify Built AI Remixes But Won't Launch Until Labels Figure Out Licensing**
Spotify's co-CEO confirmed the company has the tech ready to let fans create AI remixes and covers of their favorite artists — they're just waiting on the music industry to agree on how to split the money. For independent artists and local bands in the Valley already fighting for visibility in a platform economy that pays \$60–\$85 per 10,000 streams, this is another brick in the wall. The top 1% will monetize remixes; the other 99% will drown in more noise.

**YouTube Music Launches AI Playlist Generator to Stop Subscriber Churn**
YouTube Music rolled out an AI-powered playlist tool for premium subscribers that builds custom mixes from text or voice prompts describing moods, genres, or activities. It's not revolutionary — it's retention theater, designed to make you feel like you're getting more value so you don't cancel. For Valley businesses watching how platforms use AI to reduce churn, the lesson is simple: personalization at scale works, but only if the experience doesn't feel like a gimmick.

**800 Million ChatGPT Users Are Sending 1 Billion Referral Visits to Websites**
AI platforms are now driving over a billion referral visits to major websites, with Target seeing 40% monthly growth in traffic from ChatGPT alone. If you attended SunshineFM's AI workshops last summer at CSUSB's ERC, you learned how to get cited by these chatbots — and now the data proves it matters. For Valley businesses still treating SEO like it's 2019, the question isn't whether AI search will replace Google. It's whether your business will exist in the results when someone asks Claude or ChatGPT for recommendations.

**AI Receptionist Startup Nuvo Raises \$25M to Fix the 20–40% of Calls Small Businesses Miss**
Nuvo, an AI receptionist platform, raised \$25 million to scale human-like voice agents for small and medium businesses — targeting medical practices, restaurants, and home service companies that lose revenue every time a call goes unanswered. The company has built 15,000 AI agents to date, and early adopters report the experience is finally good enough to trust. For Coachella Valley businesses still relying on voicemail or overwhelmed front desk staff, this is the moment to stop treating phone systems like an afterthought and start treating them like digital employees.

**OpenAI Turns On Ads in ChatGPT for Free and Plus Users**
OpenAI flipped the switch on ads in ChatGPT for free and lower-tier users, a move driven by the fact that only 5% of its 800 million weekly active users pay for subscriptions while the company burns \$2.5 billion every six months. The ads are labeled, placed outside AI responses, and won't appear in conversations about health, mental health, or politics — but the trust contract just changed. For Valley users who've been treating ChatGPT like a personal advisor, therapist, or companion, the question is whether the platform still feels like it's working for you or for advertisers.

**Harvard Study: Workers Using AI Tools Are Burning Out Faster, Not Working Less**
An eight-month UC Berkeley study of 200 tech workers found that employees who adopted AI tools experienced higher burnout rates as workloads expanded rather than contracted — a phenomenon researchers call "workload creep." Workers voluntarily did more because AI made more feel achievable, with product managers writing code and researchers taking on engineering tasks, bleeding work into lunch breaks and late evenings. For Coachella Valley businesses rolling out AI tools without training or guardrails, the promise that "you'll get your afternoons back" is a lie — unless you're intentional about what productivity actually means and who it serves.

**35% of U.S. Shoppers Now Use AI Assistants, Half Would Let AI Handle Payment**
AI shopping assistant usage tripled in the past year, jumping from 12% to 35% of U.S. shoppers, with half saying they'd trust AI to handle the entire purchase process including payment. The catch: nearly 70% bail after one bad experience due to poor product recommendations. For Valley retailers and hospitality operators experimenting with chatbots and voice agents, the margin for error is razor-thin — one clunky interaction and the customer is gone.
