## Signal Drop: Friday, February 6, 2026

**Anthropic and OpenAI Drop Competing "Vibe Working" Models Within Hours**
Anthropic released Claude Opus 4.6 with agent teams and 1 million token memory (up from 200k), then OpenAI fired back with ChatGPT 5.3 Codex for "full spectrum professional work." Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleiman declared the barrier to entry officially gone — you can watch a three-minute video and start delegating entire workflows to AI. For Valley businesses still treating AI as a chatbot, this is the moment the goalpost moved from "assistant" to "employee."

**Thomson Reuters Stock Plunges 18% After Claude Cowork Demo**
When Anthropic showed how its new Claude assistant could automate legal research and contract review, Thomson Reuters suffered its largest single-day loss in company history. The investor message was brutal: if AI can vibe its way through a legal brief, what happens to companies charging thousands for that same data? Valley real estate attorneys, estate planners, and anyone selling knowledge work packaged as billable hours should be stress-testing their business models this weekend.

**IAB CEO Calls AI Scraping "Stealing," Proposes Federal Accountability Act**
David Cohen, CEO of the Interactive Advertising Bureau, told his annual conference in Palm Desert that AI companies are "free riding on the backs of publishers" and proposed the Accountability for Publishers Act to force tech giants to pay for scraped content. He's already lobbying two US Senators. The fight isn't just about news outlets — it's about every recipe blog, local report, and niche website that trained the models we now use for free. If you create content of any kind, this is your fight too.

**Apple Opens CarPlay to ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude for First Time Ever**
Apple is allowing third-party AI assistants into CarPlay while delaying Siri's overhaul until spring 2026, a rare admission that it can't win the AI race alone. OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic can now build tools for hands-free conversations in your car. For iPhone users who've been complaining about Siri for years, this is vindication. For Apple, it's a land grab concession — and a signal that even the most closed ecosystems are cracking open under AI pressure.

**Redfin and Zumper Launch AI Apps on ChatGPT's 800 Million User Homepage**
Redfin and Zumper both launched conversational search tools inside ChatGPT today, racing to become the default door for real estate on the platform's homepage. Instead of clicking checkboxes, users can now say "find me a place with a backyard, short commute, and home office space." Zumper reports AI tool usage among renters doubled in the past year from 5% to 10%. Valley real estate agents and brokers who aren't thinking about how to show up in conversational commerce are about to lose clients to whoever does.

**The Talent Stack Strategy: Stop Being a Specialist, Start Being a Stacker**
AI Breakfast's latest analysis argues the future belongs not to specialists but to "stackers" — people who combine being top 25% in three different skills rather than top 1% in one. Scott Adams (Dilbert creator) wasn't the best artist, funniest writer, or smartest businessman, but stacking all three made him irreplaceable. AI collapsed the cost of building your stack — you can now be a pretty good writer, coder, and designer all at once because AI acts as the bridge. The question for Valley workers: are you defending your old tasks or stacking new skills?

**Hospitality Firm Seeks VP of AI Innovation — A Title That Didn't Exist Six Months Ago**
A thousand-property hospitality management company is recruiting for a Vice President of AI Innovation, combining real-world hospitality experience with deep AI understanding. The role signals a shift from "AI for cost-cutting" to "AI for business model disruption." For Coachella Valley's hospitality-heavy economy — hotels, vacation rentals, event spaces — this is the canary in the coal mine. If you're not innovating, you're flat-lining, and AI is the lever that separates the two.
