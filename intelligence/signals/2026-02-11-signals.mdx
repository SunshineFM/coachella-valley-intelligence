## 2026-02-11: Signal Drop

**Google Offers Exit Packages to Employees Who Won't Embrace AI**
Alphabet sent an internal memo this week offering voluntary severance to select employees in sales, solutions, and corporate development who "may not be ready to embrace the company's accelerated AI-focused direction." Translation: if you can't keep pace with the rocket ship, here's your parachute and some equity vesting. For Palm Springs Coachella, this is an invitation — these are experienced tech workers with capital, skills, and a sudden need for a place to decompress and consider their next chapter. We have 350 days of sunshine, cheaper commercial real estate than the Bay, and a startup ecosystem that desperately needs their expertise.

**Commercial Real Estate Services Firms Plummet 15-20% on AI Disruption Fears**
CBRE, Jones Lang LaSalle, and Cushman & Wakefield all tumbled double digits Wednesday as investors rotated out of "high-fee labor-intensive business models" vulnerable to AI disruption. Zillow's weak guidance compounded the sell-off, dropping 20% after missing EBITDA expectations. For a Valley economy built on real estate transactions, development, and property management, this is a canary-in-the-coal-mine moment. The question isn't whether AI reshapes how deals get done here — it's whether local firms are experimenting now or waiting for the disruption to arrive fully formed.

**UC System Sees First CS Enrollment Decline Since Dot-Com Bust**
For the first time since the early 2000s, undergraduate computer science enrollment across the UC system has dropped — part of a national 8% pullback as students fear traditional tech career paths are evaporating. Only UC San Diego bucked the trend, the sole campus offering an undergraduate AI major. Big tech cut 127,000 jobs in 2025, reduced new graduate hiring by 25%, and Dario Amodei predicts half of entry-level white collar jobs gone in five years. If you're a parent in the Valley with a kid eyeing college, ask Cal State San Bernardino, UCR, and College of the Desert what their plan is for placing graduates in a world where the job market looks nothing like it did in 2023.

**Dono Raises Millions to Turn 3,700 County Property Registries Into Usable Data**
The US real estate market is worth $50 trillion, but it's tethered to a Frankenstein system of 3,700 county registries — pen, paper, and clunky databases that create title delays, insurance bottlenecks, and friction nobody thinks to question. Dono's AI platform transforms fragmented county records into accessible ownership data, and with 50% of the title insurance workforce expected to retire in five years, this isn't a nice-to-have. For Valley developers, investors, and anyone who's ever tried to pull portfolio data from Riverside County's public database, this is the kind of infrastructure play that quietly changes everything.

**AI Models Are Now Building Themselves — and the Feedback Loop Is Accelerating**
OpenAI's GPT-5.3 Codex documentation includes a sentence most people skimmed past: "GPT-5.3 Codex is our first model that was instrumental in creating itself." The AI debugged its own training, managed its own deployment, and diagnosed its own test results. Dario Amodei says Anthropic's AI is writing much of the company's code, and we may be one to two years from a point where the current generation autonomously builds the next. This isn't a prediction about the future — this is OpenAI and Anthropic telling you the intelligence explosion has already started. For the Coachella Valley, the implication is simple: the gap between "AI as a tool" and "AI as a substitute for cognitive work" is closing faster than any workshop or chamber lunch can keep up with.

**Matt Schumer's Viral Thread Says What I've Been Shouting for 18 Months**
Matt Schumer, CEO of HyperWrite AI, posted a 54-million-view thread on X this week that reads like a field report from the front lines: AI is no longer a helpful tool, it's doing his job better than he does, and the experience tech workers had over the past year is about to ripple through law, finance, medicine, accounting, and every other knowledge-work industry. He's not predicting — he's warning. His advice: spend one hour a day experimenting with AI, get your financial house in order, rethink what you're telling your kids, and understand that the next two to five years will be disorienting in ways most people aren't prepared for. I've been saying this in workshops, presentations, and on this show for 18 months, and the response has been polite nodding and "let's talk about better prompts." If a cybersecurity engineer working remotely for a major automaker is forwarding me this thread asking when AICV is doing more workshops, and our local universities still haven't moved on proposals sitting since September, we have a problem.
