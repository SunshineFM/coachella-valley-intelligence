**Google Offers Exit Packages to Employees Who Can't Keep Pace With AI**
Alphabet sent an internal memo yesterday offering voluntary severance to employees in its global business organization who "may not be ready to embrace the company's accelerated AI-focused direction." This isn't a layoff—it's a sorting mechanism, and it tells you everything about the speed at which legacy tech is moving. For anyone in the Valley who spent years at a major tech company and is sitting on vested equity, this might be your moment to relocate somewhere with 350 days of sunshine and build your second act. The question isn't whether to leave—it's where you go next.

**Computer Science Enrollment Drops for First Time Since Dot-Com Bust**
For the first time since the early 2000s, undergraduate computer science enrollment across the UC system has declined—except at UC San Diego, the only campus that launched an undergraduate AI major last year. Big tech companies have cut new graduate hiring by 25%, and laid-off senior developers are now competing with fresh grads for the same roles. If you're a parent in the Valley with a kid eyeing college, ask your local university leadership what their plan is for placing 20,000 students into jobs that may not exist in five years. The silence will tell you everything.

**127,000 Tech Workers Laid Off in 2025 as AI Does the Work**
US-based tech companies cut 127,000 jobs in 2025, with AI contributing to at least 55,000 of those losses according to outplacement firm Challenger Gray. A 22-year-old software engineer in the Valley told me his job search has taken six months and hundreds of applications—competing against experienced developers who were just let go. This isn't a prediction about the future of work; this is a description of what already happened last year. The only question is whether you're preparing for the next wave or pretending it won't reach you.

**MIT Estimates AI Can Already Automate 11.7% of the US Labor Market**
MIT's Iceberg Index labor simulation tool estimates that current AI systems can already automate tasks representing 11.7% of the US labor market—about 20 million workers or \$1.2 trillion in wages. Stanford found a 13% decline in employment for workers ages 22 to 25 in AI-exposed fields since 2022. The researchers are careful to say this reflects technical capability, not imminent job losses, but that distinction only matters if your company chooses not to use the technology. How long do you think that choice lasts when your competitor is moving twice as fast at half the cost?

**Commercial Real Estate Stocks Plunge 15-20% on AI Disruption Fears**
CBRE, Jones Lang LaSalle, and Cushman & Wakefield all tumbled 15-20% in a single day as investors rotated out of high-fee, labor-intensive business models viewed as vulnerable to AI disruption. It's being called an "AI scare trade," compounded by weak guidance from Zillow, which plunged 20% after missing EBITDA forecasts. For a Valley economy built on real estate development, hospitality, and property services, this should be a wake-up call. The question isn't whether AI will disrupt real estate—it's whether local firms are positioning themselves to lead that disruption or be swallowed by it.

**AI Startup Raises Millions to Modernize Nightmarish County Property Records**
A startup called Dono just raised significant funding to transform fragmented county property records into accessible ownership data—a problem anyone who's tried to pull portfolio data from 3,700 county registries knows intimately. The US real estate market is worth \$50 trillion, and it's tethered to Frankenstein systems held together by pen, paper, and outdated software. With 50% of the title insurance workforce expected to retire in the next five years, this isn't just a tech story—it's an infrastructure crisis waiting to be solved. For Valley real estate professionals, the firms that adopt these tools first will have a five-year head start on everyone else.

**OpenAI's GPT-5.3 Codex Helped Build Itself**
OpenAI released GPT-5.3 Codex last week and buried this line in the technical documentation: "GPT-5.3 Codex is our first model that was instrumental in creating itself." The AI debugged its own training, managed its own deployment, and diagnosed its own test results. Dario Amodei at Anthropic says AI is now writing much of the code at his company, and we may be one or two years away from AI autonomously building the next generation of AI. This is the intelligence explosion researchers have warned about, and it's no longer theoretical. It's happening right now, and the people building it say the feedback loop is accelerating month by month.

**71% of Americans Fear AI Will Put Too Many People Out of Work Permanently**
A Reuters Ipsos poll found that 71% of Americans fear AI will eliminate jobs permanently, and a 2026 Resume Now survey found 60% of workers believe AI will eliminate more jobs than it creates this year. Dario Amodei has publicly predicted that AI will eliminate 50% of entry-level white-collar jobs within one to five years, and many in the industry think he's being conservative. For the Coachella Valley, where hospitality, real estate, and administrative work employ tens of thousands, this isn't an abstract threat. It's a question of whether local leadership is preparing the workforce now or waiting until the layoffs start.
