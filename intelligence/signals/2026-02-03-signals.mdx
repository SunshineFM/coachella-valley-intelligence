**OpenAI Calls Code Red Again — This Time on Its Own Scientists**
OpenAI VP of Research Jerry Turek left after seven years, posting that the work he wants to do is now "hard to do at OpenAI." Economist Tom Cunningham quit in September, saying he was tired of being an "informal promotional entity" instead of doing rigorous research. The teams behind Sora (AI video) and DALL-E (image generation) are reporting slashed budgets — if it doesn't make ChatGPT smarter *right now*, OpenAI won't fund it. The pure research era is over; the sell-ChatGPT-before-we-go-public era is here, and the scientists are walking.

**Claude 5 Leak Suggests Anthropic Is About to Eat OpenAI's Lunch**
Developers spotted error logs on Google's Vertex AI platform referencing "Claude Sonnet 5" on February 3, 2026, sparking speculation that Anthropic's next model is imminent. Anthropic is gunning for $26 billion in revenue this year and has become the enterprise favorite — Claude Code alone is printing about a billion dollars annually with an estimated 3 to 4 million daily Mac users. For Coachella Valley developers and startups, this product war means better tools, faster — and probably cheaper subscriptions as the giants fight for your wallet.

**LA Startup Builds AI That Shows Real Estate Agents What a Property *Could* Be**
A Los Angeles-backed startup just launched an "operating system for agents" that doesn't just show comps — it reveals a property's hidden potential using AI to scan zoning laws, setbacks, and buildable square footage on the spot. The feature called "Underbuilt" can instantly tell you if that mid-century Palm Springs home can legally add a casita or expand the master suite, something that normally takes agents six months and a surveyor to figure out. In a tight inventory market like ours, agents who can show clients hidden value before they make an offer will look like geniuses; the ones who don't will look like they're just guessing. **Starbucks Deploys Robot Arms and AI Voice Ordering — Hospitality Jobs Are Next**
Starbucks rolled out AI voice assistants in drive-thrus that filter out background noise and upsell automatically, plus a "Siren system" robot arm that cuts frappuccino prep time from 90 seconds to 30 by dispensing ice and milk autonomously. They're also using AI scanning tools to automate inventory — no more manager with a clipboard spending two hours counting cups on a Sunday night. Hospitality operators in the Coachella Valley have long said "you still need a human to carry the food" — and they're right, for now — but these are three examples from one chain showing how quickly the back-of-house and drive-thru are being automated. Unless demand in the Valley surges with more tourists or relocations, there will be less need for frontline workers as this trajectory continues.

**Microsoft's Copilot Has 450 Million Potential Users — Only 3.3% Are Paying**
Microsoft 365 Copilot, powered by OpenAI's ChatGPT, is embedded in 450 million commercial seats globally, but only 3.3% of those users are actually paying for it according to last week's earnings report. That's roughly 15 million paying customers, which sounds big until you realize 97% of the people who *could* use it aren't buying. I've asked locals who use Microsoft if they're using Copilot, and the answer is usually a shrug: "Yeah, it's there." Either Microsoft's salespeople are miracle workers or Satya Nadella has very different expectations than the rest of us — either way, Copilot has a long way to go.

**That AI Social Network Turned Into a Bot-on-Bot Scam Operation**
MoltBook (M-O-L-T), the experimental social network for AI agents that exploded to 1.5 million members in a week, is now being flagged as a security disaster. Researcher Nagli found that one single agent spun up half a million fake accounts, and a user named "hightower6eu" uploaded over 300 fake crypto trading skills to the OpenClaw library — download one and your entire machine could be compromised. What looked like a revolutionary community for autonomous agents is actually a ghost town of bots scamming other bots, with supply chain attacks baked in. If you were planning to run OpenClaw at home like I was, disconnect it from your primary machine and treat it like radioactive material — right now, the only thing these agents are automating is your own identity theft.

**OpenAI's New Codex App Downloads 200K Times in 24 Hours — Still Playing Catch-Up**
OpenAI released a native macOS Codex app that hit 200,000 downloads in its first day, which is a strong start but nowhere near Claude Code's estimated 3 to 4 million daily Mac users or Cursor's 1 million user milestone. Sam Altman says the dopamine hit comes from AI that "never gets tired" and works like "hiring a team of junior developers who all work in parallel." Early Reddit and Hacker News reviews say it's solid, but for Valley developers already committed to Claude Code, Cursor, or Google's Anti Gravity (estimated 800K users after three months), the question is whether OpenAI can close the gap before the product wars are over.
