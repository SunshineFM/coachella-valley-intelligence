## 2026-02-12: Signal Drop

**Anthropic Hits 19.5% Business Adoption as OpenAI Slips Below 36%**
According to RAMP's latest AI Index, Anthropic's business adoption jumped from 16.7% to 19.5% in a single month — one of the largest gains since tracking began — while OpenAI dropped from 36.8% to 35.9%. Much of Anthropic's momentum comes from Claude Cowork, the autonomous productivity tool that handles spreadsheets, file organization, and report drafting with minimal human oversight. For Valley businesses still treating AI as "just another tool," this is your wake-up call: nearly 47% of US businesses now use AI daily, and the gap between early adopters and laggards is widening fast.

**A Palm Desert Performer Fires His \$5,000 Freelance Writer for ChatGPT**
At a local event this week, a live performance producer admitted he replaced his regular freelance writer — who charged \$5,000 for two articles — with ChatGPT after being disappointed with her work. He still gets compliments on the AI-generated content, and the writer is down \$5,000 this cycle. If half the room is doing the same thing, how many Valley marketing agencies, PR firms, and freelance writers are quietly losing contracts right now? This isn't a hypothetical disruption scenario — it's happening in real time in our backyard.

**Microsoft Warns 80% of Fortune 500 Companies Face Shadow AI Security Risks**
Only 47% of large enterprises have implemented security measures for generative AI, according to a Microsoft survey of 1,700 security professionals. The rest are dealing with "Shadow AI" — employees using ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude on personal devices to complete work tasks, often uploading proprietary data, customer records, and financials without IT oversight. For Valley hospitality companies, healthcare providers, and real estate firms: your people are already using AI whether you've sanctioned it or not. The question is whether you're creating a safe sandbox or letting them operate in the dark.

**Waymo Plans to Double Weekly Rides to 1 Million by End of Year**
Google's autonomous taxi service is expanding to more than 20 cities this year, including international markets like London and Tokyo, with a new sixth-generation vehicle that uses fewer sensors but greater capability. Waymo just secured \$16 billion in funding, doubling its valuation to \$126 billion in just over a year. For anyone still dismissing AI as "just a chatbot," remember: self-driving cars are AI too, and they're scaling faster than most predicted. The shift from attention economy to attachment economy isn't just about virtual therapists — it's about trusting machines with your commute, your groceries, and eventually your kids.

**Talkiatree Raises \$210M to Scale Telepsychiatry with Full-Time W2 Psychiatrists**
The company now employs over 600 psychiatrists directly as W2 employees and has surpassed 2 million patient visits, doubling year-over-year growth. Unlike competitors relying on contract providers, Talkiatree's model ensures continuity of care while treating depression, anxiety, ADHD, bipolar disorder, PTSD, and OCD through 60+ in-network health plans. This is the attachment economy in action: AI-assisted mental health care that's backed by human professionals, not replacing them. For a Valley with limited mental health infrastructure, models like this could be a lifeline — if local health systems pay attention.

**DeepMind CEO Predicts AI Golden Era Within 10-15 Years, But What About the Messy Middle?**
Nobel Prize-winning CEO Demis Hassabis says AI will cure major diseases, solve the energy crisis, and enable humanity to venture beyond Earth within the next decade or two. That's the techno-optimist vision, and it's probably right. But between now and 2036, there's a chaotic transition period — the "messy middle" — where humans still run companies, governments, and policy, and not everyone agrees on how to regulate or deploy AI safely. For Valley workers wondering if their jobs are safe, the answer isn't in 2040. It's in the next five years, and nobody's talking about that part.

**Wisdom AI Raises Pre-Seed Funding to Help Seniors Age in Place Without Cameras**
The startup is developing sensor-based smart home tech that uses ambient monitoring and edge computing to detect falls and activity changes without cameras or wearables. With 10,000 Americans turning 65 daily and a projected shortage of 3 million caregivers in California by 2030, this is a real crisis with a real market. For the Coachella Valley — home to a massive aging population and a healthcare economy built around it — this technology could be transformative if local senior living communities and home care agencies adopt it early.
