## 2026-02-12: Signal Drop

**Anthropic Jumps to 19.5% Business Adoption as OpenAI Slips**

**Microsoft Says 80% of Fortune 500 Companies Face Shadow AI Risks**
Only 47% of large companies have implemented security measures for generative AI, even as employees quietly use ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini on personal devices to get work done, according to a Microsoft survey of 1,700 security professionals. This "Shadow AI" problem — workers uploading proprietary documents, client records, and financials to consumer AI tools — is creating massive vulnerabilities. For Valley hospitality and healthcare companies handling sensitive guest or patient data, this isn't theoretical: your employees are already using AI, and you don't know what they're sharing.

**Waymo Plans to Double Weekly Rides to 1 Million by Year-End**
Google's autonomous taxi service Waymo will expand across 20+ cities this year, including London and Tokyo, after securing $16 billion in funding that doubled its valuation to $126 billion. The sixth-generation vehicles use 13 cameras, four LiDAR units, and six radars — fewer sensors than before, but with greater capability. For the Valley's aging population and sprawling geography, autonomous vehicles could solve last-mile transportation problems faster than any public transit plan, but only if local leaders start planning for infrastructure and regulatory frameworks now instead of waiting for Waymo to show up.

**DeepMind CEO Predicts AI Golden Era Within 10-15 Years — But What About the Next Five?**
Nobel Prize-winning DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis says AI will cure major diseases, solve the energy crisis, and enable humanity to venture beyond Earth within 10 to 15 years. That's inspiring, but it glosses over the "messy middle" — the 5 to 10 years of chaos as AI disrupts labor markets, regulatory frameworks struggle to keep pace, and humans fight over who controls the technology. For Valley workers and business owners, the question isn't whether AI will create abundance in 2040 — it's what you're doing in 2026 to prepare for the disruption happening right now.

**Wisdom Raises Pre-Seed Funding to Help Seniors Age in Place Using AI Sensors**
Wisdom, a new AI startup backed by the Richard King Mellon Foundation, is developing sensor-based smart home technology that monitors older adults for falls and health changes without cameras or wearables. The system uses ambient sensors and edge computing to process data locally, addressing privacy concerns while alerting caregivers when something appears wrong. This matters for the Valley: California will need 3 million caregivers by 2030, and we're already facing 1.8 million job vacancies nationwide. If AI can help seniors age in place safely, it could ease pressure on both families and the Valley's healthcare infrastructure — but only if local senior living communities and home care agencies adopt it quickly.
