## 2026-01-12: Signal Drop

**Palm Springs Median Home Price Now \$750K — Up 15% Year Over Year**

The median home price in Palm Springs just crossed \$750,000, a 15% jump from January 2025, while median household income in the Valley sits around \$60,000. That math doesn't work for teachers, hospitality workers, or the people who actually keep this place running. The question every developer and city council member should be asking: who exactly are we building this place for?

**Coachella Valley Water District Warns of "Significant Infrastructure Gaps"**

The Coachella Valley Water District quietly released a report showing aging infrastructure that can't keep pace with current development, let alone the pipeline of new projects. We're talking water lines from the 1960s serving neighborhoods that didn't exist five years ago. For anyone buying property or planning a build in the East Valley, this isn't background noise — it's a material risk to your investment and timeline.

**Short-Term Rental Permits in Palm Springs Drop 22% After New Restrictions**

Palm Springs issued 22% fewer short-term rental permits in 2025 compared to 2024, following stricter noise ordinances and neighbor complaint protocols. That's real inventory leaving the market, which means less accommodation capacity during high season and potentially higher nightly rates for the tourists who do come. If you're in hospitality or own rental property here, the regulatory environment just became your biggest variable.

**Desert Sands Unified Reports 8% Enrollment Decline Over Three Years**

Desert Sands Unified School District lost 8% of its student enrollment between 2023 and 2025, a trend that mirrors housing affordability challenges pushing families out of the Valley. Fewer kids in schools means less state funding, which means cuts to programs and staff. This isn't just an education story — it's a signal that the economic foundation of the Valley is shifting under our feet.

**CV Link Bike Path Sees 40% Usage Increase, Still Mostly Empty**

The CV Link regional bike and pedestrian path reported a 40% increase in usage in 2025, which sounds impressive until you realize it's still mostly empty most days. We spent \$100 million on 50 miles of pathway that connects our cities, and it remains a solution looking for a problem. The real question: what would it take to make car-free transit actually viable here, or did we just build very expensive sidewalks?

**Palm Springs International Airport Passenger Traffic Up 12% in 2025**

PSP processed 12% more passengers in 2025 than 2024, driven largely by new direct routes to Seattle, Denver, and Austin. More flights mean more visitors, more economic activity, and more pressure on our already strained infrastructure and housing. For local businesses, this is good news in the short term — but it also means the clock is ticking on addressing capacity issues before we hit a wall.

**Riverside County Approves 3,200-Unit Development Near Thermal**

Riverside County supervisors approved a 3,200-unit residential development near Thermal last week, adding to the 10,000+ units already in the pipeline for the East Valley. The developer promises "workforce housing," but history suggests most units will price out the actual workforce within two years. If you're tracking where the Valley is headed, watch Thermal — it's either going to be a model for smart growth or a cautionary tale.
