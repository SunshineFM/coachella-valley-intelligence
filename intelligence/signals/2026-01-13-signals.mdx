## 2026-01-13: Signal Drop

**Trump Sentenced to Unconditional Discharge in Manhattan Hush Money Case**
Donald Trump received an unconditional discharge Friday in the Manhattan hush money case — no jail, no fine, no probation — after being convicted on 34 felony counts related to falsifying business records. Judge Juan Merchan's sentencing came just 10 days before Trump's inauguration, making him the first convicted felon to assume the presidency. For Palm Springs' politically engaged snowbird population and year-round residents watching national chaos unfold, this marks a historic inflection point: the American presidency now carries a criminal record, and nobody seems sure what precedent that sets.

**Bitcoin Crashes 10% to $91K, Then Bounces Back Above $94K**
Bitcoin dropped from $102,000 to $91,000 in 48 hours before recovering to $94,500 — a $10,000 swing that vaporized and then restored billions in paper wealth. The volatility came amid broader market jitters and profit-taking after Bitcoin's recent run toward six figures. For Coachella Valley's growing crypto-curious investor class and the tech workers who've relocated here with digital portfolios, this is a reminder that the asset class remains wildly unpredictable even as it inches toward mainstream acceptance.

**Palm Springs Housing Crisis: $750K Median Price, 1.9% Vacancy, Zero Affordable Units**
Palm Springs' median home price hit $750,000 while the rental vacancy rate sits at 1.9% — functionally zero availability — and the city has exactly zero income-restricted affordable housing units. This isn't a housing shortage; it's a housing emergency that's pushing service workers, young families, and longtime residents out of the city entirely. The question every developer, city council member, and business owner should be asking: who will staff your restaurants, clean your hotels, and teach your kids when nobody making under $100K can afford to live here?

**SunshineFM Launches AI-Powered Local News Experiment**
SunshineFM is testing a fully AI-generated local news format using Claude, Perplexity, and ElevenLabs to produce daily signal drops for Palm Springs and Coachella Valley. Founder Sat is building this as an open experiment in modern media infrastructure — transparent about the AI stack, focused on local signal over national noise, and designed to serve a community that's underserved by traditional outlets. If it works, it's a template for how small markets can have sophisticated local media without the overhead of a traditional newsroom; if it doesn't, it's still a fascinating case study in what AI can and can't do for local journalism.

**Waymo Expands Autonomous Rides on LA Freeways**
Waymo is now offering fully autonomous rides on Los Angeles freeways, marking a major expansion of driverless technology into high-speed, high-complexity driving environments. The service is available to all Waymo One users in LA, removing the previous waitlist restriction for freeway trips. For Palm Springs, this matters because autonomous vehicle technology is rapidly moving from novelty to infrastructure — and a city built around car culture and tourism could see major shifts in how visitors move through the Valley once driverless rides become economically competitive with Uber and rental cars.

**Meta Ends Fact-Checking, Adopts Community Notes Model**
Meta announced it's ending its third-party fact-checking program on Facebook and Instagram, replacing it with a Community Notes system similar to X's crowdsourced approach. The company cited concerns about censorship and bias, signaling a major retreat from content moderation just as Trump prepares to return to office. For local businesses and community organizations in the Valley that rely on Facebook for reach and engagement, this means the information environment is about to get messier — and the responsibility for calling out misinformation shifts from professional fact-checkers to the crowd.
