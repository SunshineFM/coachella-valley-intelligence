## 2026-01-13: Signal Drop

**Palm Springs Median Home Price Hits \$675K While Inventory Sits at 2.9 Months**
The desert housing market is showing classic supply constraint symptoms: median sale price reached \$675,000 in December with just 2.9 months of inventory available (six months is considered balanced). For builders, developers, and anyone trying to staff a business here, this isn't just a real estate story — it's a talent retention crisis. You can't grow a local economy when your workforce can't afford to live within 30 minutes of their job.

**Trump's "Golden Dome" Federal Land Privatization Could Reshape Desert Development**
The incoming administration is floating a plan to sell federal land holdings to private buyers, potentially unlocking massive tracts around Palm Springs and the Coachella Valley currently managed by BLM. If this moves forward, it fundamentally changes the development calculus in a region where federal ownership has constrained growth for decades. The question: does this create opportunity for thoughtful infill and workforce housing, or does it just accelerate sprawl without the infrastructure to support it?

**I-10 Remains the Valley's Biggest Unpriced Externality**
Every conversation about desert growth runs into the same wall: I-10 is a parking lot, and there's no serious plan to fix it. Widening projects are incremental, rail connections are theoretical, and the economic cost of that congestion — lost productivity, reduced business access, lifestyle friction — isn't showing up in any municipal budget. For anyone building here, transportation infrastructure is the silent tax on every expansion plan.

**Coachella Valley's Water Story Is More Stable Than the Narrative Suggests**
Contrary to the "desert running dry" headlines, the Coachella Valley has functional water management through groundwater replenishment, Colorado River allocations, and State Water Project access. The real issue isn't absolute scarcity — it's allocation, cost, and the political willingness to let new development connect to existing systems. If you're evaluating a project here, water is a solvable problem, not a dealbreaker.

**Workforce Housing Is the Unlock Nobody's Funding**
The Valley has luxury product, it has retiree product, it has vacation rental product — what it doesn't have is attainable workforce housing at scale. Teachers, hospitality workers, retail staff, and service industry employees are either commuting from Banning or doubling up in rentals never designed for it. This is the infrastructure gap that actually matters for businesses trying to operate here, and it's not getting solved by market-rate developers alone.

**Local Media Collapse Leaves a Vacuum for Civic Information**
The Desert Sun is a shell, local TV is syndicated filler, and most residents get their news from Facebook groups with zero editorial standards. This isn't just a journalism problem — it's a governance problem, a transparency problem, and a trust problem. When there's no credible local information layer, rumor fills the gap, and bad policy gets less scrutiny.
