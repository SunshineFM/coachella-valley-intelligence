## 2026-01-15: Signal Drop

**Measure ULA's $5 Billion Wipeout Heads to California Supreme Court**
LA's mansion tax — Measure ULA — has collected just $439 million against a projected $900 million annual target, while wiping an estimated $5 billion in property value off the city's books. The California Supreme Court is now reviewing whether the measure violated constitutional tax rules, with oral arguments expected this spring. For desert developers watching LA's housing policy chaos, this is a cautionary tale about what happens when transfer taxes get weaponized. If ULA falls, expect a wave of stalled LA deals to suddenly move.

**Palm Springs Rent Control Gets Tighter as Vacancy Decontrol Dies**
Palm Springs just tightened its rent control ordinance by eliminating vacancy decontrol — meaning landlords can no longer reset to market rates between tenants. The city joins a growing list of California municipalities doubling down on tenant protections despite evidence that strict rent control shrinks housing supply. For small-scale landlords in Palm Springs, the math just changed: your rent roll is now a fixed asset with capped upside, making exit timing more critical than ever. This will quietly push more mom-and-pop owners toward selling to larger operators who can absorb the regulatory overhead.

**Coachella Valley Gets Its First AI-Powered Building Permit Assistant**
Cathedral City soft-launched an AI chatbot this week that walks contractors through permit applications, answers code questions in real time, and flags common errors before submission. Early users report cutting permit prep time by 40% and first-submission approval rates are up 23%. This is the kind of quiet infrastructure upgrade that compounds — faster permitting means faster construction cycles, which means more housing supply hitting the market sooner. If it works, expect every Valley city to copy it by summer.

**Desert Hospitality Wages Up 8.3% Year-Over-Year as Labor Market Tightens**
Average hourly wages for hospitality workers across the Coachella Valley hit $21.40 in December, up from $19.75 a year ago, according to new EDD data. Hotels and restaurants are competing harder for staff as Acrisure Arena events and expanded Stagecoach programming drive demand. For operators, this wage pressure is permanent — the Valley's labor pool isn't growing fast enough to match tourism expansion. The winners will be whoever figures out how to deliver premium service with 15% fewer labor hours.

**Indian Wells Tennis Garden Adds 2,000-Seat Grandstand Ahead of BNP Paribas Open**
The Indian Wells Tennis Garden just completed a $47 million expansion adding a new 2,000-seat grandstand and upgraded player facilities two months before the BNP Paribas Open kicks off in March. This marks the venue's largest capital investment since 2014 and signals confidence that the tournament can continue growing despite competition from Saudi-backed tennis ventures. For Valley businesses, BNP Paribas Open is a two-week economic engine worth roughly $400 million in regional impact — every capacity expansion is a multiplier on that number.

**Palm Desert Approves 340-Unit Workforce Housing Project on Cook Street**
Palm Desert's city council greenlit a 340-unit apartment complex on Cook Street aimed at workforce housing, with 20% of units reserved for households earning 80% of area median income. The project includes ground-floor retail and sits two blocks from the El Paseo shopping district, making it one of the few new housing developments in the Valley with actual walkability. This is the kind of infill density the region desperately needs, but it's still the exception rather than the rule. Watch whether this becomes a template or an outlier.

**Coachella Valley Water District Raises Rates 6.2% to Fund Colorado River Contingency**
CVWD approved a 6.2% rate increase effective February 1st to fund infrastructure upgrades and water banking projects as Colorado River allocations remain uncertain. The average residential customer will see bills rise by about $8 per month, while commercial users face steeper increases tied to consumption. For developers, this is another line item creeping up — water availability and cost are now permanent variables in every pro forma. The Valley's growth story is still intact, but the cost of that growth is rising faster than most people realize.
