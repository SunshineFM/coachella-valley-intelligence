**SunshineFM Goes Live as Local Media Experiment**
A new radio project called SunshineFM launched today at 1:11 PM, positioning itself as "modern media for Palm Springs Coachella" focused on AI, startups, business, culture, and ideas. The format is explicitly experimental — testing whether local media can break from traditional structures and sound more like curiosity-driven conversation than programmed content. For a region that's seen legacy media shrink while tech and creative industries grow, this is a bet that there's an audience hungry for something that sounds less like a news desk and more like the Valley itself. The question: can experimental formats find sustainable audiences, or does "modern media" just mean unfunded media?

**Radio as First Love, Podcast as Second Attempt**
The host describes a lifelong obsession with radio — from recording shows on cassette as a kid to working in college radio and commercial stations — but admits previous podcast attempts failed because they felt like "work." This launch is framed as a return to the original spark: just turning on a mic and talking, without overthinking format or audience. That tension between structure and spontaneity is the entire game in local media right now. If this works, it's because it refuses to sound like it's trying to work.

**"We'll Figure It Out" as Editorial Strategy**
The entire first episode is built around not knowing what comes next — no script, no plan, just a commitment to show up and experiment in public. The host explicitly says the format will evolve based on what feels right, not what's "supposed" to happen in media. For builders in the Valley watching this, it's a mirror: how many of your projects are stuck because you're waiting for the perfect plan instead of just turning the thing on? The risk isn't that this fails. The risk is that it stays safe.

**Local Media Framed as Infrastructure, Not Content**
SunshineFM is described as serving "Palm Springs Coachella" — a regional frame that treats the entire Valley as one connected ecosystem rather than isolated cities. That's a subtle but important editorial choice: it assumes the region's builders, creatives, and businesses share more in common than city boundaries suggest. If local media is infrastructure, not just content, then the question becomes: what does it enable people to do? This project seems to be testing whether media can be a coordination layer for a region that's growing faster than its institutions.

**AI and Startups Named as Core Coverage Areas**
The show explicitly lists AI and startups alongside culture and entertainment as core topics, signaling that tech isn't a niche interest here — it's part of the regional identity. That's a bet that Palm Springs Coachella has enough people building, investing, or working in tech-adjacent fields to justify coverage that doesn't treat it as foreign or futuristic. For a region often stereotyped as retirees and resorts, that framing is quietly radical. The question is whether the audience exists at scale, or whether this is serving a small, loud minority.

**"Modern Media" Defined by What It's Not**
The phrase "modern media" gets repeated throughout, but it's never explicitly defined — only implied through what it's rejecting: traditional radio formats, scripted shows, corporate voice, and the assumption that media needs to be polished to be valuable. That's a risky brand position because it relies on the audience understanding what you're against without being totally clear what you're for. For now, "modern" seems to mean: experimental, local, conversational, and willing to sound unfinished. Whether that's compelling or just unpolished depends entirely on execution over time.

## Local Radar

**Watch for SunshineFM's Format Evolution This Week**  
The show promised to experiment in public and figure out its structure as it goes — which means the next few episodes will reveal whether this is a sustainable format or a one-time experiment. If you're building something in public yourself, this is worth following as a case study in how (or whether) unscripted local media can find an audience.

**Track How "Palm Springs Coachella" as Regional Frame Lands**  
The decision to treat the entire Valley as one media market rather than separate cities is either smart infrastructure thinking or a mismatch with how people actually identify. Pay attention to whether the show attracts cross-Valley listeners or stays hyper-local to one area.

**Notice Which Topics Get Traction First**  
The show listed AI, startups, business, culture, and entertainment as focus areas — but it's unclear which will dominate. If you're building or investing in the region, the topics that get the most airtime will signal what the host thinks matters most (or what the audience responds to).

**Look for Guest Voices or Solo Format Lock-In**  
The first episode was solo, but the format is loose enough to accommodate guests, co-hosts, or interviews. Whether this stays a one-person show or opens up will determine its range and scalability.

**Check If This Sparks Other Local Media Experiments**  
If SunshineFM gains any traction, it could give permission to other builders in the Valley to launch their own experimental formats — newsletters, podcasts, video series — without waiting for traditional media infrastructure. The real signal might not be this show's success, but whether it catalyzes others.
