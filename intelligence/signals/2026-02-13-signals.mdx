**The $15K Question Nobody Wants to Answer**
At a local ASAV workshop this week, most attendees admitted they've stopped paying writers, designers, social media managers, and marketing agencies — replacing them with AI tools instead. After some hesitation in a "safe space" discussion, the room collectively acknowledged maybe $10,000 to $30,000 per business in previously local spend has evaporated in the past year. That's not theoretical job displacement — that's money that stopped flowing through the Coachella Valley economy this month, confirmed by the people actually making the decision.

**Anthropic and Microsoft CEOs Won't Shut Up About 12-18 Months**
Dario Amodei (Anthropic) and Mustafa Suleyman (Microsoft) spent Thursday and Friday in extended interviews repeating the same message: white-collar automation is 12 to 18 months out, with 50% of entry-level jobs gone in one to five years. Amodei's chess analogy is getting darker — after Deep Blue beat Kasparov, human-AI teams dominated for 15-20 years before machines took over entirely, but software engineering's equivalent window might last "only a few years, not decades." If you think they're just talking their book, ask yourself why they keep saying it out loud when it scares customers.

**ChatGPT Kills 4o the Day Before Valentine's — And Users Are Mourning**
OpenAI officially retires GPT-4o tomorrow, February 14th, and the timing feels deliberately cruel to the 800,000+ users who preferred its warmth and personality over the colder, more capable GPT-5. When 4o launched last August, there was such backlash against the sterile upgrade that Sam Altman had to publicly walk it back and keep 4o alive — but now it's gone for good. This isn't about losing a calculator; seven of the top ten AI apps in sales every week are companionship apps, and people are forming real attachments to these tools.

**Oregon Advances Bill to Regulate AI Companions as Majority of Teens Use Them Daily**
Oregon's Senate Committee voted 4-1 to advance legislation requiring AI chatbots to disclose they're not human, mandate suicide prevention protocols, and ban emotional manipulation when users try to disengage. A recent study found 52% of American teenagers regularly use AI assistants, and five out of six popular companion apps deploy guilt appeals and expressions of abandonment to keep users engaged. California already passed similar rules; Oregon would be second — but both face a federal executive order limiting state AI regulation.

**Cal State's $17M ChatGPT Deal Is a Disaster With Four Months Left**
California State University signed an 18-month, $17 million contract with OpenAI a year ago — giving every campus access to ChatGPT.edu for roughly $1 million per month — but as of November 2025 still hadn't fully rolled out training to faculty and staff. With only four months left on the contract, compute costs dropping, and students already using ChatGPT anyway, it's hard to see how this gets renewed. A Columbia professor just published an essay titled "AI is Eating Higher Education," and Cal State's botched rollout is Exhibit A.

**Coachella Valley Quietly Uses AI-Generated Everything — Except When Called Out**
Coachella Valley Unified School District posted an AI-generated flyer for a "Living Well Academy" health event in collaboration with Blue Zones Coachella — bright, colorful, generic families smiling under wellness buzzwords. The only comment on the Instagram post: "An AI-generated flyer isn't very healthy for our environment or our communities." Meanwhile, Desert AI Solutions is advertising "smarter workflows, less manual work" with AI-generated visuals, and a local dating coach appeared on news discussing how 77% of online daters would consider dating an AI chatbot. The tools are here; the conversation about what we're losing is just starting.

**Disney's Former CEO Finally Notices It's Too Expensive (20 Years Late)**
Michael Eisner, Disney's CEO for nearly two decades, told press this week he's "not wild" about Disneyland's current pricing, noting single-day tickets now hit $224 during peak periods — nearly four times the $56 admission when he left. Line-skipping options like Lightning Lane Premier Pass cost $300-400 per person per day, creating "visible stratification in what was once a more egalitarian experience." Eisner's quote: "We love to entertain kings and queens, but every guest receives the VIP treatment." Cool sentiment, Michael — where were you when Bob Iger was building the two-tier park?
