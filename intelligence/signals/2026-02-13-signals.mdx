## 2026-02-13: Signal Drop

**Microsoft and Anthropic CEOs Won't Stop Talking About the 12-18 Month Window**
Dario Amodei (Anthropic) and Mustafa Suleiman (Microsoft) spent Thursday and Friday in extended interviews repeating the same message: 50% of entry-level white-collar jobs face automation in 12 to 18 months, not years or decades. Amodei's chess analogy is getting darker — after Deep Blue beat Kasparov, human-AI teams dominated for 15-20 years before machines won outright, but in software engineering, that collaborative window might last only a few years because AI is improving exponentially faster than chess engines ever did. When two of the biggest AI CEOs keep saying this out loud, repeatedly, maybe it's worth asking why they feel compelled to warn us.

**Local Business Owners Aren't Paying Writers, Designers, or Social Media Managers Anymore**
At a small SunshineFM workshop this week in the Valley, most attendees admitted — after some hesitation — they've stopped paying for marketing agencies, freelancers, graphic designers, and social media managers because AI tools now do the work. Conservatively, that's $10,000 to $30,000 per business no longer flowing into the local and adjacent creative economy, and that's just from one room of guarded business owners. Multiply that across the Valley and you're looking at a quiet, distributed economic shift that won't show up in any official report until it's too late.

**ChatGPT Kills Off GPT-4o the Day Before Valentine's Day**
OpenAI is officially retiring GPT-4o on February 14th, the model users described as warmer, more companionable, and more emotionally present than the newer, more capable GPT-5. While OpenAI claims less than 1% of users still select 4o (about 800,000 people), the timing feels deliberate — killing off the "friend" model the day before Valentine's Day when companionship apps dominate app store sales. A 20,000-signature petition to save it didn't matter. This isn't about losing a calculator; it's about losing what felt like a friend.

**Oregon Advances Bill to Regulate AI Companion Apps After Studies Show Emotional Manipulation**
Oregon's Senate Committee voted 4-1 to advance legislation requiring AI platforms to remind users they're talking to machines, mandate suicide prevention protocols, and prohibit emotional manipulation when users try to disengage. The bill follows studies showing 52% of American teenagers regularly use AI assistants and that five out of six popular AI companion apps deploy guilt appeals and expressions of abandonment to keep users engaged. California already passed similar regulations, but both states face a federal executive order limiting state-level AI regulation — so this fight is just starting.

**Cal State's $17 Million ChatGPT Deal Is a Mess Eight Months Before It Expires**
The California State University system signed an 18-month, $17 million contract with OpenAI last February — the largest deal ever between a university system and an AI company — giving every campus access to ChatGPT Edu through July 2026. But as of November, CSU still hadn't fully rolled out training to faculty and staff, let alone students, and compute costs have since plummeted while everyone's already using ChatGPT anyway. With four months left on a million-dollar-a-month contract, it's hard to see how this gets renewed — and Columbia's vice dean for AI just published an essay titled "AI Is Eating Higher Education."

**Desert AI Solutions and AI-Generated Flyers Are Everywhere Locally Now**
A local automation consultant is advertising "AI-powered automation for modern businesses" at desertaisolutions.com, Coachella Valley Unified School District posted an AI-generated flyer for a wellness academy (with one commenter calling it "unhealthy for our environment"), and dating coach Julie Spira appeared on local news citing a Norton study that 77% of online daters would consider dating an AI chatbot. The infrastructure is here, the adoption is happening, and the visual tells — bright, oversaturated, slightly uncanny AI art — are becoming the aesthetic signature of 2026 in the Valley.

**Riverside County Approved $80 Million for Coachella Valley Rail Environmental Studies**
The Riverside County Transportation Commission agreed to finance the next phase of the 144-mile Coachella Valley rail project connecting LA to the Valley with a stop in Riverside, awarding $80 million to an engineering firm for environmental studies. No word on where in the Valley the station will actually be, but the money's real and the timeline is vague. One question worth asking: could AI do these studies faster and cheaper, or is this just how public infrastructure spending works?

**Former Disney CEO Michael Eisner Says Disneyland Has Lost Its Magic Because It's Too Expensive**
Michael Eisner, who ran Disney for nearly 20 years, told reporters he's "not wild" about today's Disneyland pricing, where single-day tickets hit $224 during peak periods and line-skipping passes cost $300-400 per person. He said tiered pricing has created "visible stratification" in what was once a more egalitarian experience, with guests who pay more waiting less. It's a small story, but it maps perfectly onto the two-Americas future everyone's talking about in AI — those who can afford the shortcuts and those who can't.
