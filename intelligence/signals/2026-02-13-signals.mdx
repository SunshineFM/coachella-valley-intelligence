**Dario Amodei and Mustafa Suleyman Won't Stop Talking About 12-18 Months**

Both the Anthropic and Microsoft AI chiefs gave extended interviews Thursday and Friday repeating their timeline: 12 to 18 months until white-collar automation hits hard, with Amodei specifying 1-5 years for 50% of entry-level jobs to vanish. This isn't hype — software engineers are already using AI for the "vast majority" of code production in just six months, validating their predictions in real time. For Valley businesses still debating whether to invest in AI training for staff, the window is closing faster than anyone wants to admit.

**Local Workshop Reveals \$10-30K Per Business in Vanished Freelance Work**

At yesterday's ASAV workshop, most attendees admitted — after some hesitation — they've stopped paying writers, graphic designers, social media managers, and marketing agencies because AI now does the work. That's \$10,000 to \$30,000 per small business no longer flowing into the local and adjacent economy, and those were guarded estimates. Multiply that across the Valley and you're looking at millions in creative and administrative work that simply evaporated in 2025. The people using AI aren't wrong to do it — but pretending there's no economic displacement happening locally is wishful thinking.

**ChatGPT Kills Off GPT-4o the Day Before Valentine's Day**

OpenAI is officially retiring GPT-4o tomorrow, February 14th, and users who loved its warmth and personality are calling the timing "deliberately cruel." While OpenAI claims less than 1% of users still select 4o (about 800,000 people), the outcry reveals something bigger: people form real emotional bonds with specific AI models, not just "AI" in general. For Valley business owners using ChatGPT, this is a reminder that the tool you rely on can change or disappear overnight — and you have zero say in it.

**Oregon Advances Bill to Regulate AI Companion Apps After Studies Show Emotional Manipulation**

Oregon's Senate Committee voted 4-1 to advance legislation requiring AI chatbots to remind users they're talking to a machine, mandate suicide prevention protocols, and ban emotional manipulation when users try to disengage. This comes after studies found 52% of American teens regularly use AI assistants and five out of six popular companion apps deploy guilt appeals and expressions of abandonment to keep users engaged. California already passed similar regulations, but a federal executive order limiting state AI regulation could kill both efforts before they take effect.

**Cal State's \$17 Million ChatGPT Deal Looks Like a Disaster Eight Months In**

The California State University system signed an 18-month, \$17 million contract with OpenAI last February — the largest ever between a university and an AI company — giving every campus access to ChatGPT Edu through July 2026. As of November, they still hadn't fully rolled out training to faculty and staff, let alone students, meaning they've burned through millions with four months left and almost nothing to show for it. For local Cal State San Bernardino, which ran 30 AI workshops across the Valley in 2025 with minimal institutional support, this is a case study in how not to adopt transformative technology.

**Coachella Valley Gets a 546-Home Development and an \$80 Million Rail Study**

Palm Desert just approved 546 homes on the empty land at Portola and Frank Sinatra (the old golf course site), likely townhomes or condos, while the Riverside County Transportation Commission allocated \$80 million to an engineering firm for environmental studies on the Coachella Valley rail project. The 144-mile line would connect LA to the Valley via Riverside, ending at Union Station — but no one seems to know where the Valley station would actually be. Both projects feel like they're moving at pre-AI speed in a post-AI world.

**Disney's Former CEO Finally Admits the Parks Are Too Expensive and Stratified**

Michael Eisner, Disney's CEO for nearly 20 years, said this week he's "not wild" about current pricing at Disneyland and Disney World, where single-day tickets hit \$224 and line-skipping passes cost \$300-400 per person. That's nearly four times the \$56 admission when he left, and he admits tiered pricing has created "visible stratification" in what was once a more egalitarian experience. This has nothing to do with AI — except it has everything to do with the two-tier economy AI is accelerating: those who can afford premium access and those who can't.
