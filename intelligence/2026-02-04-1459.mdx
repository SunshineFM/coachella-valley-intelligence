**\$285 Billion Erased From Software Stocks After Anthropic Launches Claude Cowork**

Anthropic released 11 new plugins for Claude Cowork last week — AI agents that autonomously review legal contracts, manage sales pipelines, and analyze data without humans clicking through traditional software. Wall Street panicked, wiping \$285 billion off software stocks in a single day, according to Bloomberg and CNBC. For Coachella Valley businesses heavy on real estate transactions, estate planning, and back-office admin work, this is both threat and opportunity: local firms could handle 10x volume with the same staff, but hourly billing models for routine document work are now under siege. The question isn't whether this changes your software stack — it's when you start experimenting before your competitors do.

**OpenAI Retires GPT-4o on February 13, Users Revolt Over "Unhealthy Attachments"**

OpenAI is permanently retiring the GPT-4o model on February 13 — just before Valentine's Day — and CEO Sam Altman claims users exhibit "unhealthy attachments" to the warmer, more conversational tone of the older model. Users are canceling subscriptions and protesting the loss of what they describe as a "two-year companion," while OpenAI insists usage has shifted to the newer GPT-5.2 model. For Valley businesses using ChatGPT to write emails, social posts, or customer service responses, this matters: if you're still on 4o, your tone will change next week whether you like it or not. This is the first time software has created parasocial relationships without requiring a human on the other end — and the industry has no idea how to handle it.

**Timmy the Service Robot Starts Work at Hilton Garden Inn in Rancho Mirage**

A fully autonomous robot named Timmy — built by TechForce Robotics — is now operating 24/7 at the Hilton Garden Inn in Rancho Mirage, changing linens, delivering items, transporting luggage, and handling waste. The company says staff feedback has been "overwhelmingly positive" after a year of testing at company-owned hotels, with employees viewing it as a tool that reduces physical strain rather than a job threat. But here's the business reality: hospitality margins are thin, and the biggest line item is always payroll — if Timmy saves 5-10% on labor costs, that's where the ROI lives, no matter what the press release says. The question for Valley hospitality leaders is how long before "supporting staff" becomes "replacing staff" when the next budget cycle hits.

**First SAG-AFTRA Actress Stars in Professional AI-Generated Horror Film**

Independent horror filmmaker B. Harrison Smith and Friday the 13th actress Lauren Marie Taylor are releasing *Slow Burn* on February 14 — the first professional AI-generated feature film starring a recognized SAG-AFTRA actress, created using HiLuo's Minimax AI video model. Smith claims he wrote a traditional script and "directed the AI brush" to build scenes around Taylor's performance, positioning it against low-effort "AI slop" by using traditional film disciplines like blocking, lighting, and continuity. The timing is deliberate: SAG-AFTRA negotiations on AI performer protections are happening this month, and this film is a proof-of-concept that will complicate those talks. For the Coachella Valley's creative community — which has talked for decades about becoming a Hollywood satellite studio without the talent density to pull it off — AI tools like Minimax might finally make that possible.

**Christopher Nolan Reports 40% Drop in Hollywood Guild Membership, Blames AI Job Losses**

Director Christopher Nolan revealed that membership in a major Hollywood guild (either DGA or SAG-AFTRA) has dropped 40%, attributing much of the decline to AI-driven job losses alongside other industry challenges like runaway production. This comes as AI video tools like HiLuo's Minimax and Google's World Engine (from Emerge Labs) are enabling professional-grade filmmaking at a fraction of traditional costs. For the Valley's creative community — which includes directors, writers, and below-the-line talent with second homes behind country club gates — this is both crisis and opportunity: if Hollywood work is evaporating in LA, could the desert finally become a viable production hub with 350 days of sunshine and AI-enabled workflows that don't require massive crew density?

**Cal State San Bernardino Warns Students About AI-Generated Resume Sameness**

Cal State San Bernardino is hosting an event (March 12 or 19, depending on which page you trust) warning students that generative AI tools have made job applications so generic that recruiters are now "inundated with hundreds of identical resumes." The event promises to teach "proven job search strategies" and "productive ways of using AI" — which sounds like Job Search 101 with an AI sticker slapped on. The deeper issue: what colleges teach and what workplaces need is a chasm that widens daily, and events like this don't bridge it unless they're teaching actual AI fluency, not just "don't let ChatGPT write your resume." For Valley students entering a job market where recruiters also use AI to filter applications, standing out requires skills that go beyond avoiding generic cover letters.
