January was a month of public failure at SunshineFM. Not the polished kind where you learn three lessons and package them into a LinkedIn carousel. The real kind — where AI agents go rogue, systems break overnight, and you wake up to find the thing you built yesterday doesn't work today.

Sat Duggal, the station's program manager, spent the entire month building in public: AI producers, songwriters, assistants, program schedulers. The promise of AI is that it can do almost everything. The reality is that if you have just enough technical knowledge to be dangerous but not enough to keep the agents on rails, you end up with chaos. And that's exactly where SunshineFM has been operating — in the gap between what AI can theoretically do and what it actually does when you try to use it for something that matters.

This isn't a polished podcast. It's not professional radio. Duggal has seen what actual radio stations run — the staff, the infrastructure, the coordination required to keep a signal live. SunshineFM is none of that. It's one person and a stack of AI tools, trying to create something that sounds like media but is built entirely differently. The experiment is whether that's even possible. And if January is any indication, the answer is: barely, and only if you're willing to look incompetent most of the time.

## LinkedIn Is Now Entirely AI

Duggal made an offhand observation that landed harder than probably intended: LinkedIn is now almost entirely AI-generated content, including the comments. If you've been on the platform recently, you know this is true. The posts have a certain sameness. The engagement feels synthetic. The comments are suspiciously on-brand and weirdly generic at the same time. It's not that people aren't posting — it's that the line between human and machine output has blurred to the point where you can't tell anymore, and maybe that's the point.

This is the environment SunshineFM is being built in. A world where AI-generated content is everywhere, most of it bad, and the bar for "good enough" keeps dropping because volume beats quality in the attention economy. Duggal's bet is that you can use AI differently — not to spam LinkedIn, but to build actual infrastructure. To create a media platform that does things a single human couldn't do alone, without pretending the AI isn't there.

The honesty is the strategy. Most people using AI right now are trying to hide it. SunshineFM is doing the opposite — making the AI visible, making the failures visible, and asking whether that transparency creates something more interesting than the polished fake stuff flooding every other platform.

## The Pitch to Bay Area Money

Duggal's goal for 2026 is to convince friends from the Bay Area that when they get rich — not if, when — they should move to Palm Springs Coachella instead of Miami, Austin, or Utah. This is not a pitch based on infrastructure. There's no deep talent pool here. No major universities. No established investor class. No startup ecosystem to speak of.

The pitch is simpler: 350 days of sunshine and blue skies. That's it. That's the only guarantee SunshineFM offers. Everything else is a build. The startup ecosystem doesn't exist yet, but it could, and the people who move here early get to shape what it becomes. Duggal is betting that some subset of newly wealthy tech founders will choose quality of life and blank-slate potential over plugging into an existing scene.

It's a long bet. But it's also the only bet that makes sense for a place like Palm Springs Coachella, which has incredible lifestyle advantages and zero institutional infrastructure for the kind of people Duggal is trying to attract. You can't compete with San Francisco or Austin on their terms. You have to offer something different. And if that thing is "build the future in the desert with 350 days of sun," maybe that's enough for the right people.

## What Public Building Actually Looks Like

The theme of this episode — and of SunshineFM's entire January — is that building with AI in public is not clean. It's not a smooth ramp from prototype to product. It's a series of things breaking, getting fixed, breaking again in a different way, and slowly, painfully, becoming slightly more stable.

Duggal is not hiding this. The station is explicitly "not polished" and "not professional." It's an experiment in whether one person with AI tools can create something that feels like a media company. And the answer, so far, is: sort of, sometimes, if you're okay with it falling apart regularly.

This is actually useful information. Most people talking about AI are either selling you something or summarizing research papers. Very few are saying "I tried to build a thing and here's what actually happened." SunshineFM is doing that. The station is the experiment. The failures are part of the content. And for anyone else trying to figure out what AI can actually do in 2026 — not in a demo, but in production — this is one of the few places showing the real middle part.

The question is whether that's enough. Whether a media platform built this way can become something people actually care about, or whether it stays a curiosity. Duggal is betting it can. And the only way to find out is to keep going, keep breaking things, and keep showing up every day with another episode, another song, another attempt at making this work.

That's the bet. That's the build. And that's what February is going to test.
