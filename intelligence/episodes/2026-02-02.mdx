January 2026 was a month of public failure. Every day, something broke. An AI agent went rogue. A workflow that worked yesterday stopped working today. This is what it actually looks like to build with AI right now — not the polished demos, not the investor decks, but the daily grind of trying to get these systems to do what you want them to do without enough technical knowledge to fully control them.

SunshineFM has been running this experiment live since the start of the year. AI agents handle the heavy lifting: producers, songwriters, assistants, content creators. The station program manager — that's still human — but almost everything else is delegated to systems that are powerful, unpredictable, and occasionally useless. The promise of AI is that it can do almost everything. The reality is that it can do almost everything, but not reliably, not consistently, and not without constant supervision.

This is not professional radio. It's not a polished podcast. It's an experiment in what happens when you stop waiting for AI to be perfect and just start building with it in public. The staff at traditional radio stations is large for a reason. The production work is real. The coordination is real. The consistency is hard. SunshineFM is trying to replace most of that with AI, and the result is messy, uneven, and occasionally brilliant.

## The LinkedIn Problem

If you've been on LinkedIn lately, you've noticed something. Almost everything feels AI-generated. The posts, the comments, the engagement — it's all starting to blur together. This might be a function of who you follow, but it's also a function of how easy it's become to automate the appearance of thought leadership. You can generate a post in seconds. You can generate comments in seconds. You can generate an entire professional presence without ever having a single original idea.

This is the risk of AI in content: not that it's bad, but that it's good enough to flood the zone with mediocrity. The challenge for platforms like SunshineFM is to use AI for the grunt work — the scheduling, the production, the formatting — while keeping the editorial voice sharp, specific, and human. That's the balance. That's the experiment.

## The Pitch to Bay Area Founders

The goal for 2026 is simple: convince several friends from the Bay Area that when they hit their exits and become super wealthy, they should consider Palm Springs Coachella. Not Miami. Not Austin. Not Utah. Here.

The pitch is not about talent density. It's not about universities. It's not about an existing investor class or a mature startup ecosystem. None of that is here yet. The pitch is about building it. About being early. About creating the thing that doesn't exist yet.

When founders ask what SunshineFM can guarantee, the answer is honest: 350 days of sunshine and blue skies. That's it. No infrastructure promises. No ecosystem guarantees. Just weather, space, and the opportunity to build something from scratch.

This is a long-term play. The startup ecosystem that Palm Springs Coachella needs doesn't exist yet. SunshineFM is trying to create it by being the media platform that makes it visible, that tells the story, that attracts the people who want to build it. This is not a passive strategy. This is an active bet that the right people, given the right conditions, will choose to build here instead of somewhere else.

## AI Music and the Palm Springs Air Museum

SunshineFM is also a record label now. The first AI artist on the label is Rani, whose track "Checking In" opened and closed the broadcast. The station also co-wrote and co-created a song for the Palm Springs Air Museum called "Silver Wings and Satin Shoes." This is what AI music looks like in practice: not replacing human musicians, but creating new kinds of content for specific local institutions and audiences.

The Air Museum is one of the most distinctive cultural assets in the valley. It's a place where history is tangible, where you can stand next to a P-51 Mustang or a B-17 and feel the weight of what those machines represented. Creating music for that institution is a way of connecting AI-generated content to something deeply local, deeply specific, and deeply human.

This is the model: use AI to create content that serves local institutions, local stories, and local identity. Not generic content. Not content that could be about anywhere. Content that is about here.

## What It Means to Build in Public

Building in public means showing the failures. It means admitting when something doesn't work. It means not pretending that the technology is more reliable than it is. SunshineFM has been doing this all month, and the result is a broadcast that is uneven, experimental, and occasionally frustrating.

But it's also honest. And in a media environment where most AI content is either overhyped or hidden behind closed doors, there's value in showing the actual process. The daily grind. The things that break. The things that work. The things that almost work but need another iteration.

This is what it looks like to build a media platform with AI in 2026. It's not seamless. It's not polished. But it's real, and it's happening here.
