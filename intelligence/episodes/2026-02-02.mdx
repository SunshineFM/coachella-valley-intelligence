LinkedIn is now almost entirely AI-generated content, including the comments. If you're active there, you've probably noticed. If you haven't, you're either not paying attention or you're part of the problem.

This isn't speculation. This is what Sam — station program manager at SunshineFM — is seeing firsthand while building an entire radio station where AI agents do the heavy lifting. And January 2026 was the month he decided to do all of it in public, failures included.

## What It Actually Looks Like to Build With AI Right Now

All of January, SunshineFM has been running AI agents as producers, songwriters, assistants, and program managers. Not as a polished demo. Not as a proof of concept with cherry-picked results. As the actual operational infrastructure of a media platform broadcasting from Palm Springs Coachella.

And it's been a mess.

Things go up one day and fall apart the next. Agents go rogue. They don't do what they're supposed to do. Sam has enough technical knowledge to be dangerous but not enough to keep the systems from breaking. This is the reality of working with AI in early 2026 — it can do almost everything, but "almost" is doing a lot of work in that sentence.

The station isn't professional radio. It's not a polished podcast. It's an experiment in what happens when you don't wait for the technology to be perfect before you start building. And if you're using AI for anything beyond writing emails and LinkedIn posts, you know exactly what this feels like.

Most people aren't there yet. Most people are still using AI as a better autocomplete. But the gap between "write my social media post" and "run my entire content operation" is where the actual learning happens. It's also where everything breaks.

## Why This Matters for Palm Springs Coachella

SunshineFM's goal for 2026 is explicit: convince several friends from the Bay Area that when they become super wealthy, they should consider Palm Springs Coachella. Not Miami. Not Austin. Not Utah. Here.

Not because of the talent pool — that's not here. Not because of the universities — those aren't here either. Not because of the investor class or the established startup ecosystem. None of that exists in Palm Springs Coachella right now.

But it could.

The pitch is simple: come here and help build the startup ecosystem that doesn't exist yet. And when people ask what SunshineFM can guarantee, the answer is honest: 350 days of sunshine and blue skies. That's it. That's the only guarantee.

It's a strange pitch. It's also the only honest one. You don't move to Palm Springs Coachella because the infrastructure is already there. You move here because you want to build it, and you want to do it somewhere that isn't San Francisco or New York or the other places where the infrastructure is already calcified.

This is the same reason people moved to Miami in 2020 and 2021. It wasn't because Miami had a mature tech ecosystem. It was because it didn't, and a few people decided that was an opportunity rather than a problem.

## The AI Artist on the Record Label

SunshineFM now has its first AI artist on its record label. Her name is Rani. The track is called "Checking In." It's a long one — an extended edition, Sam says, though he meant to say "extended version."

This isn't a novelty. This is the station testing what it means to have an AI-generated artist as part of the roster. Not as a gimmick. As an actual artist with a catalog and a presence and a relationship with the audience.

The station also co-wrote and co-created a track for the Palm Springs Air Museum called "Silver Wings and Satin Shoes." This is what it looks like when a media platform becomes a production engine — not just broadcasting content, but creating it, commissioning it, and tying it directly to local institutions.

The Air Museum isn't a random choice. It's one of the most distinctive cultural assets in the valley. A track written for it is a signal about what kind of content SunshineFM is building — hyper-local, tied to specific places and institutions, and willing to experiment with format.

## What Happens When You Build Before It's Ready

The entire premise of SunshineFM is that you don't wait for AI to be perfect. You build with it now, in public, and you let people see the failures alongside the successes.

This is uncomfortable. Most media companies — most companies, period — don't build this way. They wait until the technology is stable, until the product is polished, until they can control the narrative. SunshineFM is doing the opposite. Every breakdown is visible. Every pivot is documented. Every rogue agent is part of the story.

This approach only works if you're clear about what you're doing. SunshineFM is explicit: this is an AI experiment in content co-creation between humans and artificial intelligence. It's not trying to be NPR. It's not trying to be a traditional radio station. It's trying to figure out what a media platform looks like when AI does most of the work, and it's doing that work in a place — Palm Springs Coachella — that doesn't have an existing media infrastructure to compete with.

That's the advantage. There's no legacy system to dismantle. There's no entrenched audience expecting a specific format. There's just space to try things and see what works.

## The Bet on Who Moves Here Next

The real bet SunshineFM is making isn't about AI. It's about who moves to Palm Springs Coachella in the next few years.

If the people who get rich from the current wave of AI companies decide to move somewhere new, where do they go? Miami is crowded now. Austin is expensive. Utah is cold. Palm Springs Coachella has sunshine, space, and nothing to lose by letting people experiment.

SunshineFM is building the media infrastructure for that future. Not because it's guaranteed to happen. Because someone has to build it before it happens, and no one else is doing it.

That's the only pitch that matters. Not the one about what's already here. The one about what could be here if the right people show up and decide to build it.
