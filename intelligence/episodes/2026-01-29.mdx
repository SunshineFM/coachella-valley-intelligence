Oracle and MGX are still in, but the command structure flipped in 72 hours.

OpenAI isn't repositioning a half-trillion-dollar project for press release purposes. They're doing it because someone has to figure out where to put the datacenters, who runs the power lines, and how to cool chips that generate heat like small suns. ## DeepSeek Forced Every AI Lab to Recalculate

DeepSeek's R1 model launched last week and caused every major AI lab to reprice their API offerings within days. The Chinese lab built a frontier-class reasoning model for $5.6 million — a number that made no sense until people started testing it and realized it actually performed.

OpenAI dropped their API prices. Google dropped theirs. Anthropic is expected to follow. This isn't a sale. This is a repricing of what intelligence costs when someone figures out how to train models more efficiently. DeepSeek used a distillation method that compressed the reasoning capability of much larger models into something that runs cheaper and faster.

The technical term is "distillation." The practical term is "we've been overcharging." DeepSeek proved you don't need a $100 million training run to get frontier performance on reasoning tasks. You need better methods. The entire AI industry just learned that compute efficiency matters more than compute scale, and every company selling API access had to adjust their pricing before customers switched.

For anyone in Palm Springs Coachella building on AI tools, this is the moment costs drop and capability expands. The models you couldn't afford last month are now accessible. The models you were using are about to get cheaper or better. This is the kind of market correction that makes new products possible.

## The Diplomatic Blowback No One Predicted

Colombia's president agreed to accept deportation flights from the US, then reversed within hours and refused them. Trump responded by threatening 50% tariffs. Colombia's president reinstated acceptance of the flights. The entire cycle took less than a day.

Then Denmark's prime minister called Trump's Greenland annexation talk "completely unacceptable." Trump responded by canceling a state visit and threatening tariffs on Denmark. This is the second European ally in a week facing tariff threats over diplomatic disagreements.

The pattern is clear: tariffs are now the primary diplomatic tool, deployed in hours, not months. This isn't trade policy. It's real-time economic pressure applied to foreign policy disputes. The speed is what's new. Previous administrations took weeks to craft tariff responses. This administration does it in a Truth Social post.

For businesses in Palm Springs Coachella that import goods or rely on international supply chains, the risk isn't tariffs themselves. It's the unpredictability. A diplomatic incident in Copenhagen can mean 50% cost increases on Danish imports by afternoon. There's no time to hedge. The only strategy is diversification — multiple suppliers, multiple countries, multiple logistics paths.

## Meta's Orion Glasses Are the First AR Device That Might Actually Ship

Meta showed Orion AR glasses to developers this week. Not a concept. Not a render. Actual working prototypes that overlay digital information on the physical world without requiring a phone tether. The glasses use a neural wristband for input, which sounds like sci-fi until you realize Meta has been working on wrist-based EMG controls for three years.

The important detail: Meta says these will ship to consumers. Not in 2026, but soon enough that they're showing them to developers now so apps exist at launch. This is the first AR glasses project from a major company that looks like it might actually reach market instead of dying in the lab.

Apple has a Vision Pro that costs $3,500 and weighs enough that you don't wear it outside. Meta has glasses that look like glasses and cost whatever Meta decides to subsidize them down to. If Meta can get Orion to $500 or $700, they own the AR market before Apple ships their rumored glasses in 2027.

For Palm Springs Coachella, this is about what happens when AR becomes ambient. When tourists navigate downtown without looking at their phones. When technicians repair HVAC systems with manuals overlaid on the equipment. When retail workers see inventory data floating next to products. The infrastructure for that is being built now.

## The Prosus Sale That Signals China Tech Exposure Is Over

Prosus, the Dutch investment giant, is selling its entire $14 billion stake in Trip.com. This is the same company that made a fortune on Tencent. They're not selling because Trip.com is failing. They're selling because European investors are done with China exposure.

The sale follows years of regulatory crackdowns in China, delisting threats in the US, and now tariff uncertainty that makes Chinese tech stocks too volatile for institutional portfolios. Prosus is getting out while the exit is still clean. Other firms will follow.

This matters because it's capital reallocation on a massive scale. $14 billion doesn't disappear. It goes somewhere else. Probably US tech, probably AI infrastructure, probably companies building in stable regulatory environments. For founders in Palm Springs Coachella raising capital, this is a better fundraising environment than it was six months ago. The money that was parked in Chinese tech is looking for new homes.
