OpenAI just announced a \$40 billion data center project called Stargate, and if you're running a business in the Coachella Valley, this isn't abstract tech news. It's a signal about where capital is flowing, what infrastructure is being built, and what kinds of capabilities will be available to you in the next 18 months.

The project involves SoftBank, OpenAI, Oracle, and MGX — a sovereign wealth fund from Abu Dhabi. They're building physical data centers in the United States to train and run AI models at scale. The first facility is already operational in Texas. This is real money going into real infrastructure, not vaporware.

Why does this matter here? Because the gap between what's possible with AI and what local businesses are actually using is enormous. Most conversations about AI in Palm Springs still center on whether to use ChatGPT for social media captions. Meanwhile, the infrastructure being built right now will enable things like real-time video generation, voice agents that sound completely human, and models that can analyze your entire business operation and tell you exactly where you're losing money.

The Stargate announcement came the same week that DeepSeek — a Chinese AI lab — released a model that reportedly cost \$6 million to train and performs comparably to models that cost hundreds of millions. That's the part that should make you sit up. If a scrappy team can build something that competitive for that little money, the cost curve for AI capability is dropping faster than anyone predicted.

For context: OpenAI's GPT-4 reportedly cost over \$100 million to train. DeepSeek claims their R1 model cost \$6 million and matches or exceeds GPT-4 on many benchmarks. Whether those numbers are perfectly accurate or not, the direction is clear. AI capability is getting cheaper, faster.

## What This Means for Local Operators

If you're running a hotel, a restaurant, a real estate brokerage, or a retail operation in the valley, you're about to have access to tools that were science fiction two years ago. Voice agents that can handle reservations, answer questions about your property, and upsell packages without ever sounding robotic. Video tools that can generate marketing content from text prompts. Models that can read your P&L and tell you which menu items are actually profitable once you account for labor and waste.

The question isn't whether this technology will exist. It already does. The question is whether you'll be early enough to use it as a competitive advantage or late enough that it's just table stakes.

One thing that's not being talked about enough: the infrastructure gap. Stargate is being built because the current supply of compute — the raw processing power needed to run these models — is constrained. Oracle, one of the partners in Stargate, already operates data centers. SoftBank has capital. OpenAI has the models. MGX brings sovereign wealth. This is a consortium designed to solve a bottleneck.

That bottleneck affects you. Right now, if you want to use the best AI models, you're often hitting rate limits, waiting in queues, or paying premium prices because supply is tight. Stargate and projects like it will ease that. Which means the cost of using frontier AI will drop, and the speed will increase.

## The DeepSeek Wild Card

DeepSeek's release is the other side of the story. They're not building \$40 billion data centers. They're building efficient models that run on less hardware. Their approach is fundamentally different: instead of throwing more compute at the problem, they're finding algorithmic shortcuts.

If DeepSeek's claims hold up, it means the moat around AI capability is narrower than the big labs want you to believe. It also means that smaller companies, regional players, and even individual developers will have access to models that were previously only available to well-funded startups.

For Palm Springs Coachella, this is good news. It means you don't need to be a venture-backed tech company to use cutting-edge AI. You just need to be willing to learn how it works and apply it to your specific problem.

## The Local Angle

The Coachella Valley economy runs on hospitality, real estate, events, and increasingly, remote workers who moved here during the pandemic and stayed. Every one of those sectors has obvious AI applications that are now economically viable.

Hospitality: voice agents for booking, dynamic pricing models that adjust in real time based on demand signals, personalized marketing that actually works because it's trained on your guest data.

Real estate: automated property descriptions, virtual staging, lead qualification, market analysis that updates daily instead of quarterly.

Events: logistics optimization, attendee engagement tools, real-time sentiment analysis from social media to adjust programming on the fly.

Remote workers: many of them are already using AI tools daily. They're your customers. They expect businesses here to be at least as sophisticated as the tools they use for work.

## What You Should Do

If you're not experimenting with AI yet, start small. Pick one repetitive task in your business and see if an AI tool can handle it. Customer service emails. Social media scheduling. Data entry. Something low-risk where failure doesn't break anything.

If you're already using AI, start thinking about infrastructure. Where is your data? Can you feed it into a model? Do you own your customer data, or is it locked in a platform you don't control? The businesses that will win in the next phase are the ones that have clean, accessible data and the willingness to let AI operate on it.

And if you're building something new — a startup, a side project, a new division of an existing business — assume that AI will be part of the stack from day one. Not as a nice-to-have feature, but as a core capability. The cost and capability curves are moving in your favor.

The Stargate announcement is a bet that AI infrastructure is a strategic asset worth \$40 billion. The DeepSeek release is a reminder that scrappy efficiency can compete with massive capital. Both things can be true. And both things matter for what you're building here.
