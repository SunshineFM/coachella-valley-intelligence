The most important thing that happened yesterday wasn't the ceremony. It was the 1,500 pardons.

Trump signed blanket clemency for nearly everyone convicted in the January 6th Capitol attack — people who assaulted police officers, who broke through barricades, who forced the evacuation of Congress. The message is simple: if you act in service of Trump's power, consequences don't apply. That's not a policy position. That's how power works now.

This matters in Palm Springs Coachella because the rules we thought governed American life — the ones that made long-term investment rational, that made contracts enforceable, that separated personal loyalty from institutional authority — are being rewritten in real time. If you're building a business here, if you're making decade-long bets on property or infrastructure or talent, you need to understand that the ground has shifted.

## The End of American Exceptionalism

For 80 years, America's global position rested on a specific claim: we weren't just powerful, we were *good*. We built institutions. We kept our word. We defended allies not because it was profitable but because it was right. That story is over.

Trump spent his inauguration day threatening to seize Greenland and the Panama Canal. Not as negotiating positions — as stated intentions. He suggested Canada should become the 51st state. He promised tariffs on allies and adversaries alike. And he did all of this while European leaders sat in the audience, watching the country that once anchored the Western alliance announce it no longer believes in alliances at all.

The world heard it. So did the markets. Bitcoin hit new highs because people are moving money into assets that don't depend on American institutional stability. Gold is up. The dollar's long-term strength as a reserve currency is now a live question.

For anyone in the Coachella Valley with international business ties — hospitality groups, real estate investors, tech founders with global customers — this is the new operating environment. American credibility is no longer an assumption you can build on.

## What's Actually Happening at the Border

Trump declared a national emergency at the southern border and mobilized the military. He's designating cartels as terrorist organizations, which sounds tough until you realize it's a legal framework that allows military action inside Mexico without calling it a war.

He also ended birthright citizenship by executive order. That's unconstitutional on its face — the 14th Amendment is explicit — but the point isn't legality. The point is to force the issue into courts he's spent years packing, and to create chaos in the meantime.

Here's what that means locally: the Coachella Valley has one of the largest undocumented populations in California. Agriculture, hospitality, construction — entire sectors depend on workers who are now being told their children, born here, are not citizens. The legal battles will take years. The economic disruption starts immediately.

If you run a business that depends on labor, you need a plan for what happens when ICE enforcement ramps up and workers stop showing up. If you're in housing, you need to think about what happens when families start leaving. This isn't abstract federal policy. It's coming to the valley.

## The Regulatory Bonfire

Trump signed an order imposing a federal hiring freeze and requiring agencies to cut two regulations for every new one they issue. He put Elon Musk in charge of finding cuts, which means the world's richest man — someone who benefits directly from deregulation in automotive, aerospace, and AI — is now deciding which rules apply to his own industries.

The EPA is being gutted. The Department of Education is on the chopping block. The IRS is losing enforcement staff, which means tax compliance becomes optional for anyone with good lawyers.

For founders and builders in Palm Springs Coachella, this creates opportunity and risk in equal measure. If you're in solar, in water tech, in anything that depended on federal climate or infrastructure funding, your grants and tax credits are now uncertain. If you're in industries that were heavily regulated — cannabis, real estate development, fintech — you might suddenly have more room to move.

But here's the thing: deregulation doesn't mean freedom. It means the rules are now made by whoever has the most leverage in the room. If you're not Elon Musk, that's probably not you.

## The Pardon Power as Political Weapon

Trump didn't just pardon January 6th defendants. He's signaling that the pardon power is now a tool of political loyalty. If you're useful to him, you're protected. If you're not, you're exposed.

That changes the calculus for anyone in business or politics. The question is no longer "what does the law say?" It's "who do you know?" That's how systems work in places without strong institutions. We're becoming one of those places.

## What This Means for the Valley

Palm Springs Coachella has always been a place people come to when they're done with somewhere else. That's about to accelerate. If you're building here, you're building in a region that will attract people fleeing instability — political, economic, environmental. That's opportunity. It's also pressure on housing, water, infrastructure, schools.

The question is whether local leadership can build systems that work when federal systems don't. Can the valley create its own stability — in energy, in water, in governance — when the national picture is chaos?

Because the national picture is chaos. And it's not temporary. This is the new normal.

The American idea — the one about rule of law, about institutions that outlast individuals, about being a country and not just a territory controlled by whoever wins — is over. What comes next is being written right now. And it's being written by people who believe power is the only thing that matters.

If you're building something that lasts, you need to build it knowing that.
