## The Math Has Stopped Working

For 50 years, real productivity has been flat. Birth rates are dropping. Fewer workers, no efficiency gains — the fundamental equation of economic growth is broken. Then three years ago, this current wave of AI arrived.

AI Breakfast's recent analysis frames it clearly: AI isn't here to break the labor market. It's here to fix the math. It's the lever we need just as the workforce starts to shrink. And if you've been feeling like the economy is a treadmill that keeps getting faster while you stay in the same place, you're not imagining it.

The stock market lost nearly a trillion dollars in value this week, mostly on fears that AI will dismantle the software-as-a-service economy. We've already lost about a million jobs to AI since 2025. But job hiring overall will stay flat — not because AI isn't powerful, but because it's redistributing tasks, not eliminating roles outright.

The real question: are you defending your old tasks, or are you stacking new skills?

## Stop Being a Specialist. Become a Stacker.

Scott Adams, creator of Dilbert, popularized the talent stack concept years ago. His argument: trying to be the best in the world at one thing is a losing game. The odds of becoming an Olympic athlete or a Nobel Prize-winning anything are microscopic. But being in the top 25% of three different things? That's achievable.

Adams wasn't the best artist, the funniest writer, or the smartest businessman. But he was pretty good at all three. Stack funny plus drawing plus business knowledge, and you get Dilbert.

AI collapses the cost of building your stack. You can now be a pretty good writer, a pretty good coder, and a pretty good designer all at once because AI acts as the bridge. The gap between good and great is about to become a canyon. If you use AI to just do your job, you'll be fine for a while. If you use AI to orchestrate — to build a one-person empire where you handle strategy and AI handles execution — you become something rare. And capitalism rewards rarity.

Think of your job as a container. Inside that container are tasks. Thirty years ago, executives dictated letters to secretaries who typed, mailed, and filed them. When email arrived, the typing task moved to the executive. Did the secretary disappear? Not right away. The role evolved into executive assistant, focused on higher-leverage work.

AI is unbundling your day right now. It takes the research, the formatting, the basic coding, the busy work. Strip away every task a smart machine could do in 10 seconds. What's left? That remainder is your actual value. Are you spending enough time on it?

Final question: If you could pick two skills outside your current title to add to your stack, what would they be? What happens if you combine your current expertise with data analysis? With economic research? With media production?

The future doesn't belong to the person defending a title. It belongs to the person building the combination.

## Vibe Working: The Next Phase After Vibe Coding

Anthropics and Microsoft executives declared a new phase this week: vibe working. If vibe coding changed how we build apps — you describe the vibe, AI builds it — vibe working is about to change how we do everything else.

Scott White, Anthropic's head of product, told CNBC on Wednesday: we're moving from chatting with a bot to delegating to an agent. Vibe coding was for builders. Vibe working is for knowledge workers — lawyers, accountants, analysts.

Instead of asking AI to write an email, you hand it a workflow: review these 20 contracts, flag the tax risks, update our internal spreadsheet. Then you walk away. The AI executes end-to-end with almost zero oversight.

The timing isn't an accident. This week saw a clash of titans. Anthropic released Claude Opus 4.6 — a beast with agent teams that coordinate on complex projects with a 1 million token memory. Current Claude 4.5 maxes out at 200,000 tokens. The new model can read an entire library of company data without breaking a sweat.

Minutes later, OpenAI fired back with ChatGPT 5.3 Codex, designed for full-spectrum professional work — slides, spreadsheets, research, all at once. Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleiman said the barrier to entry is officially gone. You can watch a three-minute video and start vibe working immediately.

While the tech CEOs were unveiling this on CNBC and Bloomberg, the stock market panicked. When Anthropic showed how its new Claude Cowork assistant could automate legal research and contract review, Thomson Reuters — the giant of legal and financial data — saw its stock plunge 18%, the largest single-day loss in the company's history.

The message from investors was clear: if an AI can vibe its way through a legal brief, what happens to the companies that charge thousands for that same data?

## The Publishing Industry Calls It Stealing

David Cohen, CEO of the Interactive Advertising Bureau, dropped a bomb at their annual conference here in Palm Desert at the JW Marriott. He said AI isn't innovation — it's stealing. Those are fighting words.

Every news article, every recipe, every local report you read online — AI models like ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, and Google Gemini use that data to learn. Are they paying the people who actually wrote it? AI companies can scrape the entire internet without asking. Are content creators being fairly compensated?

Cohen's solution: the Accountability for Publishers Act, a proposed law that would force tech giants to pay up. He's already shopping it to two US senators. His message is simple: no publishers, no content. No content, no internet.

It's not just about AI. Cohen says we're moving into the agentic world. This whole decade we talked about cookies and privacy. This year we're talking about AI agents doing the shopping for us. Agent-to-agent commerce is here. ChatGPT and Google Gemini went all-in on agentic commerce in January 2026.

Cohen is worried about the connective tissue between you and your digital bot. He's pushing for new standards in how we measure ads on connected TV because right now it's a mess of different sizes and rules.

Bottom line: he has to protect what ground his members still have, what monetization revenue they can still get. You and I as consumers benefit from getting free content. We're used to paying zero. I've seen you skip from one app to another when you hit a paywall. I see you sharing Netflix passwords.

It's a mess. I don't know what happens when content creators aren't fairly compensated. We've been watching the slow decay of media for a decade. This is another tectonic shift. The big AI foundational models have already been found guilty of scraping the entire internet without permission. The New York Times is suing, and honestly, I hope they win.

But when the value you create gets stripped away by AI agents, it's something to take seriously. The IAB says the free ride is over. The jury is still out.

## Apple Opens the Walled Garden — Slightly

For the first time ever, Apple is allowing third-party AI assistants like ChatGPT to take over CarPlay. OpenAI's ChatGPT, Google's Gemini, and Anthropic's Claude have all been given permission to create tools and integrations with CarPlay. Soon you'll be asking for directions and having full hands-free conversations with the smartest AI agents on the planet — not Siri.

Why is this a big deal? Apple never shares. Siri has been the only voice in the car. And Siri is not keeping up. While Apple delays the overhaul of Siri until spring 2026 — around May or June, maybe around the Palm Springs AI Tech and Creativity Expo at the convention center — customers are restless. They've been telling Apple executives: Siri sucks. We don't want to wait.

So Apple is letting the bots into CarPlay. They haven't given up entirely. They're working on a secret project codenamed Campos, plus an answer engine called Knowledge. But Apple realized they can't win this alone. Opening up CarPlay admits that for complex tasks, they just can't do it. So let ChatGPT or Google Gemini handle it.

If you're an iPhone user and you've been waiting for this, congratulations. If you've got an opinion about where Apple dropped the ball on AI, reach out. Let's get you on the air.

## Real Estate Moves Into ChatGPT

Redfin and Zumper officially moved into ChatGPT this week. Redfin launched their AI app today. Zumper beat them by a few hours.

The way you used to search: set a price, click three bedrooms, hope the map didn't glitch. Those days are over. Now you just talk to your bot. Tell it: find me a place with a backyard, a short commute to downtown, and enough room for a home office.

Redfin's senior VP of product, Ariel Dos Santos, says this is about making search conversational rather than mechanical. Conversational — really? Back in 2015, I was part of several tech startups building conversational commerce. We absolutely believed the future of the internet was going to be voice. We spent millions developing skills for Alexa, projects for Google Assistant, even for Siri. We built systems where you'd talk to your car while driving, it would answer your emails, place food orders by the time you got home. It was all conversational commerce.

Eleven years later, this executive is talking about conversational rather than mechanical. We knew this was the way it was going.

This is part of a massive wave. ChatGPT has 800 million weekly active users. If you were in our workshops last year, you heard us say: you have a small window of opportunity as a small startup or small business in Coachella Valley. You can fight the big boys. Here's one way: get on the front page of ChatGPT. Easier said than done now, but a year ago it was easier. We still have the thesis of how it should be done and where the puck is going. If you're interested in getting your business or startup on the front pages of the LLMs, reach out.

This isn't just for buyers. Zumper is targeting renters. They believe they're the first rental-only platform in the ChatGPT store. Did you know ChatGPT had a store? Zumper's data shows the number of renters using AI tools more than doubled in the last year — from about 5% to about 10%.

This isn't a gimmick. It's a land grab. Since OpenAI opened its app store late last year, the race is on to see who becomes the default door for various sectors. Redfin is leaning on the idea that they want to be the everything app for your mortgage and your move. They're seeing where the puck is going: we consumers may not automatically go to Google or Zillow. We may just be talking to our phones, to our cars. And when you're at that moment of asking a question, ChatGPT or Google Gemini or Claude or Perplexity will be on the other end, suggesting the brands and companies you should consider doing business with.

If you're a real estate agent, broker, or in commercial real estate and you want to know how to use this technology and get on the front pages, reach out. AICV has boot camps coming up this year that will take you beyond the basics.

## VP of AI Innovation: The Title That Matters

I was recruited this morning for a position I won't get — it's a pretty big company with a thousand properties under management. They're the first hospitality company I've seen with the title Vice President of AI Innovation. The magic word is innovation.

There are only three ways AI is going to take you to the next level. Two will help you now. One will keep you around for the future.

You can use AI to curtail expenses. You can use AI to get more revenue. And the third — the one barely uttered, barely used — is how you can use AI to totally disrupt yourself, to totally change your business models, to change the way your industry does things.

We call that innovation.

The headhunter told me they're looking for somebody with real-world hospitality experience along with real-world tech and AI understanding. There are very few of those people around. If you're not innovating, you're flatlining. For the next few years, AI is your opportunity to start thinking differently about how you do your work, how you treat your employees, and how you treat your customers. AI can benefit everybody in your ecosystem if used well.
