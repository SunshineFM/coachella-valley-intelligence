For 50 years, real productivity — the actual juice of the economy — has been flat. Birth rates have been dropping. Fewer workers. Those workers aren't getting more efficient. The math is fundamentally broken.

Then three years ago, this current crop of AI arrived. And according to a recent analysis from AI Breakfast, AI isn't here to break the labor market. It's here to fix the math. It's the lever we need just as the workforce is starting to shrink.

The stock market lost almost a trillion dollars in value this week, mostly because of fears that AI is dismantling the software-as-a-service economy. If SaaS startups start losing business because of AI, they start not necessarily letting go of people — though we've lost about a million jobs due to AI since 2025 — but job hiring generally will be flat. And here's why that matters for anyone building or working in Palm Springs Coachella.

## Stop Being a Specialist. Start Stacking.

The old advice was to specialize. Become the best at one thing. That's a losing game now. The odds of being an Olympic athlete or a Nobel Prize-winning coder are microscopic. But being in the top 25% of three different things? That's achievable.

Scott Adams, creator of Dilbert, popularized the talent stack concept. He wasn't the best artist, the funniest writer, or the smartest businessman. But he was pretty good at all three. When you stack funny plus drawing plus business knowledge, you get success.

AI has collapsed the cost of building your stack. You can now be a pretty good writer, a pretty good coder, and a pretty good designer all at once because AI acts as the bridge. This is what I've been battling with all year trying to build Sunshine FM — a one-person radio station with an army of AI superpowers.

Here's the truth: AI does not flatten the playing field. It magnifies it. The gap between good and great is about to become a canyon. If you use AI to just do your job, you'll be fine for a while. If you use AI to orchestrate, to build your one-person empire where you handle the strategy and AI handles the execution, you become something rare. And capitalism rewards rarity.

Final question: Are you spending your time defending your title, your salary, your place in the company? Or are you stacking new skills? Because the math of the future only works if you're willing to move.

## From Vibe Coding to Vibe Working

In the last 24 hours, the goalpost moved. Executives from Microsoft and Anthropic declared a brand new phase: Vibe Working.

Vibe Coding was the idea that you don't need to know Python or C++ anymore. You describe the vibe of the app you want, and AI builds it. We've been talking about this for over a year in our workshops. But Vibe Working is different. Anthropic's head of product, Scott White, explained to CNBC on Wednesday: we're moving from chatting with a bot to delegating to an agent.

Instead of asking AI to write an email, you hand it a workflow. Review these 20 contracts, flag the tax risks, update our internal spreadsheet. Then you walk away. The AI executes the task end to end with almost zero oversight.

The timing is not an accident. This week Anthropic released Claude Opus 4.6 — a beast with agent teams that coordinate on complex projects with a 1 million token memory. Right now the token limit on Claude 4.5 is 200,000. I know this firsthand because I ran into that limit this morning building workflows for the station. 4.6 increases that memory to 1 million tokens. It can read an entire library of your company's data without breaking a sweat.

Minutes later, OpenAI fired back by unveiling ChatGPT 5.3 Codex, designed to handle slides, spreadsheets, research — all at once. Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleiman said the barrier to entry has officially gone. You can watch a three-minute video and start Vibe Working immediately.

While the tech CEOs were announcing this on CNBC and Bloomberg, the stock market panicked. When Anthropic showed how its new Claude Cowork assistant could automate legal research and contract review, Thomson Reuters — the giant of legal and financial data — saw its stock plunge 18%, the largest single-day loss in the company's history. The message from investors was clear: if an AI can vibe its way through a legal brief, what happens to the companies that charge thousands for that same data?

## The Publishing Industry Fights Back

Imagine building a house only for someone else to move in, change the locks, and start charging rent. That's how David Cohen, CEO of the Interactive Advertising Bureau, described what AI companies are doing to publishers at the IAB's annual conference here in Palm Desert at the JW Marriott.

He dropped a bomb: it's not innovation, it's stealing. AI companies are free-riding on the backs of publishers. Every news article, every recipe, every local report — AI models like ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Google Gemini use that data to learn. Are they paying the people who actually wrote it?

Cohen's solution: the Accountability for Publishers Act, a proposed law that would force tech giants to pay up. He's already shopping it to two US Senators. His message is simple: no publishers, no content. No content, no Internet.

It's not just about AI. Cohen says we're moving into the agentic world. This whole decade we've been talking about cookies and privacy. This year we're talking about AI agents doing the shopping for us. Agent-to-agent commerce is here. ChatGPT and Google Gemini went all in on agentic commerce in January 2026.

The IAB is looking to say the free ride is over. I think the jury is out on that one. But when content creators are not fairly compensated for their work, we've been seeing the slow decay of media for about a decade. This is just another tectonic shift that's going to make publishing and media either hold on to their current IP — like the New York Times is doing with their lawsuits — or collapse entirely.

## Apple Opens the Walled Garden

For the first time ever, Apple is allowing third-party AI assistants like ChatGPT to take over CarPlay. OpenAI's ChatGPT, Google's Gemini, and Anthropic's Claude have all been given permission to create tools and integrations with CarPlay. Soon you'll be asking for directions and having full hands-free conversations with not Siri, but the smartest AI agents on the planet.

Why is this a big deal? Apple never shares. Siri has been the only voice in the car, and Siri is not keeping up with the rest of the AI assistants. While Apple delays the overhaul of Siri until spring 2026 — around Coachella, maybe around my birthday in May, maybe around June when the Palm Springs AI Tech and Creativity Expo is meant to happen again at the convention center — customers are restless. They've been telling Apple executives: Siri sucks. I don't want to wait.

Apple hasn't given up entirely. They're working on their own secret project codenamed Campos, plus an answer engine called Knowledge. But Apple realized they can't win this alone, so they've partnered up. Opening CarPlay basically admits that for complex tasks, they just can't do it. So let ChatGPT or Google Gemini do it.

## The Real Estate Land Grab on ChatGPT

Redfin and Zumper officially moved into ChatGPT this week. Redfin launched its AI app today. Zumper beat them to the punch by just a few hours.

The way you used to search: set a price, click three bedrooms, hope the map didn't glitch. Those days are over. Now you just talk to your bot. Find me a place with a backyard, a short commute to downtown, and enough room for a home office.

Redfin's senior VP of product, Ariel Dos Santos, says this is about making search conversational rather than mechanical. It's part of a massive wave because we're talking about an ecosystem of 800 million weekly active users — that's what ChatGPT has.

If you were in our workshops last year, you heard us say: you have a small window of opportunity as a small startup or small business in Coachella Valley. You can fight against the big boys. Here's one way of doing it: get on the front page of ChatGPT. Easier said than done now, but a year ago it was easier than it is today.

This isn't just for buyers. Zumper is targeting renters. They believe they're the first rental-only platform in the ChatGPT store. And people are actually using it. Zumper's data shows the number of renters using AI tools more than doubled in the last year from about 5% to about 10%.

This isn't just a gimmick. It's a land grab. Since OpenAI opened its app store late last year, the race is on to see who becomes the default door for various sectors. In this case, the race is on to become the default door for real estate.

If you're a real estate agent in town, a broker, anybody in commercial real estate, and you want to know how to utilize this technology and get on the front pages, reach out. AI Coachella Valley has boot camps coming up this year that will take you beyond the basics.

## The VP of AI Innovation

I was recruited this morning for a position I won't get — it's a pretty big company with a thousand properties under management. They're the first hospitality company I've seen with the title Vice President of AI Innovation.

The magic word is innovation. If you've come to our workshops, you know I constantly say there are only three ways AI is going to take you to the next level. You can use AI to curtail expenses. You can use AI to get more revenue. And the third — the one that's barely uttered, barely used — is how you can use AI to totally disrupt yourself, totally change your business models, the way your industry does things.

That's innovation.

When I spoke to the headhunter this morning, she told me they're looking for somebody with real-world hospitality experience along with real-world tech and AI understanding. There are very few of those people around. If you're not innovating, you're flatlining. For the next few years, AI is your opportunity to start thinking differently about how you do your work, how you treat your employees, and how you treat your customers.

Because AI can benefit everybody in your ecosystem if used well.
