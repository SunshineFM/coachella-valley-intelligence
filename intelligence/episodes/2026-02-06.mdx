The economy is a treadmill that keeps getting faster while you stay in the same place. For 50 years, real productivity — the actual juice of the economy — has been flat. Birth rates have been dropping. Fewer workers. Those workers aren't getting more efficient. The math is fundamentally broken.

Then three years ago, this current crop of AI arrived.

AI Breakfast's recent analysis suggests AI isn't here to break the labor market. It's here to fix the math. It's the lever we need just as the workforce is starting to shrink. The stock market lost almost a trillion dollars in value this week mostly because of fears that AI will dismantle the software-as-a-service economy. We've lost about a million jobs to AI since 2025. But job hiring generally will be flat — not because companies are kind, but because the math demands it.

The future doesn't belong to specialists anymore. It belongs to stackers.

## The Talent Stack Revolution

Scott Adams, creator of Dilbert, popularized the talent stack concept. He argued that trying to be the best in the world at one thing is a losing game. The odds of becoming an Olympic athlete or a Nobel Prize-winning coder are microscopic. But being in the top 25% of three different things? That's achievable. Adams wasn't the best artist, the funniest writer, or the smartest businessman — but he was pretty good at all three. When you stack funny plus drawing plus business knowledge, you get success.

AI collapsed the cost of building your stack. You can now be a pretty good writer, a pretty good coder, and a pretty good designer all at once because AI acts as the bridge. If you could pick two skills outside your current title to add to your stack, what would they be? What happens if you combine your current expertise with data analysis or economic research?

AI does not flatten the playing field. It magnifies it. The gap between good and great is about to become a canyon. If you use AI to just do your job, you'll be fine for a while. If you use AI to orchestrate and build your one-person empire — where you handle strategy and AI handles execution — you become something rare. Capitalism rewards rarity.

Are you spending your time defending your title, your salary, your place in the company? Or are you stacking new skills? Because the math of the future only works if you're willing to move.

## Vibe Working Is Here

Vibe coding changed how we build apps. You don't need to know Python anymore — you describe the vibe of the app you want, and AI builds it. Now the goalpost moved again. Microsoft and Anthropic executives are declaring a new phase: vibe working.

Anthropic's head of product, Scott White, explained to CNBC on Wednesday that we're moving from chatting with a bot to delegating to an agent. Vibe coding was for builders. Vibe working is for knowledge workers — lawyers, accountants, analysts. Instead of asking AI to write an email, you hand it a workflow: review these 20 contracts, flag the tax risks, update our internal spreadsheet. Then you walk away. The AI executes end to end with almost zero oversight.

The timing isn't an accident. This week Anthropic released Claude Opus 4.6 — a beast featuring agent teams that coordinate on complex projects with a 1 million token memory. Right now Claude 4.5's token limit is 200,000. I hit that limit this morning building workflows for the station and had to pay for extra usage. 4.6 increases that memory to 1 million tokens. It can read an entire library of your company's data without breaking a sweat.

Minutes later, OpenAI fired back by unveiling ChatGPT 5.3 Codex, designed to handle slides, spreadsheets, and research all at once. Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleiman said the barrier to entry has officially gone. You can watch a three-minute video and start vibe working immediately.

While the tech CEOs were announcing this on CNBC and Bloomberg, the stock market panicked. When Anthropic showed how its new Claude cowork assistant could automate legal research and contract review, Thomson Reuters — the giant of legal and financial data — saw its stock plunge 18%, the largest single-day loss in the company's history. The message from investors was clear: if an AI can vibe its way through a legal brief, what happens to the companies that charge thousands for that same data?

If you want to know the future, follow the money. Trillions of dollars are being invested in AI startups, data centers, and compute. Promise and peril. AI detects Alzheimer's and dementia earlier than humans can — that's the promise. Everything else you're fearful of is the peril.

## Publishing's Last Stand

Imagine building a house only for someone else to move in, change the locks, and start charging rent. That's how David Cohen, CEO of the Interactive Advertising Bureau, described AI's relationship with publishers at their annual conference at the JW Marriott in Palm Desert this week. He dropped a bomb: it's not innovation, it's stealing.

Every news article, every recipe, every local report you read online — AI models like ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, and Google Gemini use that data to learn. Are they paying the people who actually wrote it? AI scrapes the entire Internet without asking. Are content creators being fairly compensated?

Cohen's solution: the Accountability for Publishers Act, a proposed law that would force tech giants to pay up. He's already shopping it to two US Senators. His message is simple: no publishers, no content. No content, no Internet.

It's not just about AI. Cohen says we're moving into the agentic world. This whole decade we've been talking about cookies and privacy. This year we're talking about AI agents doing the shopping for us. ChatGPT and Google Gemini went all in on agentic commerce. It's real now — released in January 2026.

Cohen is worried about the connective tissue between you and your digital bot. He's pushing for new standards in how we measure ads on connected TV because right now it's a mess of different sizes and rules. The IAB has to protect what ground publishers still have, what monetization revenue they can still get.

You and I as consumers benefit from getting free content. We're used to paying zero. I've seen you skip from one app to another when you hit a paywall. I see you sharing Netflix passwords. It's a mess. I don't know what happens when content creators aren't fairly compensated. We've been watching the slow decay of media for a decade. This is another tectonic shift that will make publishing and media either hold on to their current IP — like the New York Times is doing with lawsuits — or collapse entirely.

The big AI foundational models have already been found guilty of scraping the entire Internet without permission. But we're voting with our wallets and our thumbs. When the value you create gets stripped away by AI agents, it's something to take seriously.

## Apple Opens the Walled Garden

For the first time ever, Apple is allowing third-party AI assistants like ChatGPT to take over CarPlay. OpenAI's ChatGPT, Google's Gemini, and Anthropic's Claude have all been given permission to create tools and integrations with CarPlay. Soon you'll be asking for directions and having full hands-free conversations with not Siri but the smartest AI agents on the planet.

Why is this a big deal? Apple never shares. Siri has been the only voice in the car, and Siri is not keeping up with the rest of the AI assistants. While Apple delays the overhaul of Siri until spring 2026 — around Coachella, maybe around my birthday in May, maybe around when the Palm Springs AI Tech and Creativity Expo happens again at the convention center — customers are restless. They've been telling Apple executives for quite some time: Siri sucks.

Apple hasn't given up entirely. They're working on their own secret project codenamed Campos, plus an answer engine called Knowledge. But Apple realized they can't win this alone. Opening up CarPlay admits that for complex tasks, they just can't do it. So let ChatGPT or Google Gemini do it.

## The Real Estate Land Grab

Right now when you're using Redfin or Zillow, you're scrolling through photos, clicking checkboxes. The same old way we've been using mobile phones for a long time. That's over.

Redfin and Zumper officially moved into ChatGPT today. Redfin just launched their AI app, and Zumper beat them to the punch by just a few hours. Now you can just talk to your bot. Tell it: find me a place with a backyard, a short commute to downtown, and enough room for a home office.

Redfin's senior VP of product, Ariel Dos Santos, says this is about making search conversational rather than mechanical. Conversational — really? In 2015, I was part of several tech startups on the board of advisors. I started my own tech startups around conversational commerce. We absolutely believed back then that the future of the Internet was going to be voice. We came up with so many great startups developing skills for Alexa and projects for Google Assistant and even for Siri. Millions went down that startup road. One of the things was you talking to your car — this is before Android Auto or CarPlay was fully integrated. It was all conversational commerce.

So it's interesting that 11 years later, this executive is talking about conversational rather than mechanical. We knew this is the way it was going to go.

This is part of a massive wave. We're talking about an ecosystem of 800 million weekly active users — that's what ChatGPT has. If you were in our workshops last year, you heard us say you have a small window of opportunity as a small startup or small business in Coachella Valley. You can fight against the big boys. Here's one way: get on the front page of ChatGPT. Easier said than done now, but a year ago it was easier than it is now. We still have the thesis of how it should be done and where the puck is going. If you're interested in getting your business or startup on the front pages of the LLMs, reach out.

This new technology isn't just for buyers. Zumper is targeting renters. They believe they're the first rental-only platform in the ChatGPT store. Did you know ChatGPT actually had a store? Zumper's data shows the number of renters using AI tools more than doubled in the last year from about 5% to about 10%.

This isn't just a gimmick. It's a land grab. Since OpenAI opened its app store late last year, the race is on to see who becomes the default door for various sectors. In this case, the race is on to become the default door for real estate. Redfin is leaning on the idea that they want to be the everything app for your mortgage and your move. They're seeing that where the puck is going is where we consumers may not automatically go to Google, may not automatically go to Zillow. We may just be talking to our phones, to our cars. And when you're at that moment of asking a question, ChatGPT or Google Gemini or Claude or Perplexity or xAI will be on the other end having a conversation with you and suggesting the brands and companies you may want to consider doing business with.

If you're a real estate agent in town, a broker, anybody in commercial real estate, and you want to know how to utilize some of this technology and how to get on the front pages, reach out. AICV has a couple of boot camps coming up this year that will help you beyond the basics.

## Innovation or Flatline

I was recruited this morning for a position I won't get because it's a pretty big company. They have a thousand properties under their management. They're the first hospitality company I've seen with the title Vice President of AI Innovation. Magic word: innovation.

If you've come to our workshops, you know I constantly go on about there being only three ways AI is going to take you to the next level. Two of them will help you now. One of them will keep you around for the future. You can use AI to curtail expenses. You can use AI to get more revenue. And the third — the one that's barely uttered, barely used — is how you can use AI to totally disrupt yourself, totally change your business models, the way your industry does things. We call that innovation.

When I was speaking to the headhunter this morning, she told me they're looking for somebody with real-world hospitality experience along with real-world tech and AI understanding. There are very few of those people around. This new job at a huge hospitality management firm has the title I'm so excited about: VP of AI Innovation.

If you're not innovating, you're flatlining. For the next few years, AI is your opportunity to start thinking differently about how you do your work, how you treat your employees, and how you treat your customers. AI can benefit everybody in your ecosystem if used well.

The math broke 50 years ago. AI is here to fix it. The question is whether you're defending your old tasks or stacking new skills.
