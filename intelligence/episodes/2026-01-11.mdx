OpenAI just announced a $500 billion infrastructure project called Stargate, and the entire AI industry is pretending this is normal. It's not. This is the largest private technology investment in American history, backed by SoftBank, Oracle, and MGX. The initial funding is $100 billion. They're building data centers in Texas first, then expanding nationwide. Sam Altman says this is about keeping AI leadership in the United States, which is diplomatic language for "we're in an arms race and the other side just made a move we didn't see coming."

That move is DeepSeek, a Chinese AI lab that released R1 — a reasoning model that performs comparably to OpenAI's o1 — for $5.50 per million tokens. OpenAI charges $15 for the same workload. DeepSeek trained their model for under $6 million. OpenAI reportedly spent over $100 million on o1. This isn't incremental improvement. This is a different cost structure entirely, and it changes what's possible for companies building outside the Bay Area oligopoly.

For Palm Springs Coachella, this matters in specific ways. If you're running a hospitality business and want to build custom AI for guest services, the difference between $15 and $5.50 per million tokens is the difference between "maybe next year" and "let's prototype this month." If you're a healthcare provider thinking about AI-assisted diagnostics, Chinese efficiency in model training means the technology gets cheaper faster than anyone projected six months ago. The desert has always attracted people building things the rest of California considers impractical. Cheaper AI makes more impractical things practical.

Stargate is the counter-move. Half a trillion dollars says the United States will outspend the efficiency problem. Oracle's Larry Ellison is involved, which means this is also about cloud infrastructure lock-in at national scale. They're not just building data centers. They're building the physical layer that every AI company will depend on, and they're building it with federal government cooperation that didn't exist two years ago.

## Why This Show Exists

Local news is collapsing everywhere, but it's worse in mid-sized markets. Palm Springs Coachella has 500,000 people across the valley. We have The Desert Sun, which is now owned by Gannett and produces a fraction of the coverage it did a decade ago. We have some blogs. We have Facebook groups where people argue about traffic on Highway 111. What we don't have is a dedicated intelligence layer for people building businesses, making investment decisions, or just trying to understand what's coming.

SunshineFM is that layer. It's AI-native, which means the economics are different from traditional media. There's no newsroom overhead. No printing costs. No legacy distribution contracts. Just research, synthesis, and broadcast. The format is radio because radio is the original ambient intelligence — you can listen while driving through the desert, while working, while doing anything else. The content is whatever matters: AI developments that change cost structures for local business, entertainment industry moves that affect our production economy, federal policy that touches desert communities, cultural shifts that show up here before they show up elsewhere.

This is an experiment in what modern media looks like when you start from scratch in 2025. No paywalls. No ads yet. No venture capital pressure to scale before the model works. Just daily intelligence for a specific place.

## What Else Happened This Week

CES happened in Las Vegas, which is close enough to matter. Nvidia announced new GPUs. Every car company announced AI features. The pattern is clear: AI is moving from research labs into products faster than the regulatory environment can adapt, which means early adopters get advantages that compound. Palm Springs has always been a place where early adopters come to build — that's why we have more mid-century modern architecture per capita than anywhere else. The people who moved here in the 1950s were early adopters of desert living. The people moving here now are early adopters of remote work, climate migration, and lifestyle businesses that don't require coastal proximity.

The entertainment industry is also shifting. Netflix announced new production spending. Apple is reportedly pulling back. The Coachella Valley has a growing production economy — commercials, music videos, some TV. When streaming platforms change their content budgets, it shows up in location scout activity six months later. Worth watching.

Perplexity launched a new reasoning model. Google is reportedly accelerating Gemini development. Anthropic is quiet, which usually means they're building. The AI race is no longer about who has the best model. It's about who has the best distribution, the lowest costs, and the most defensible infrastructure. Stargate is a bet that infrastructure is the moat. DeepSeek is a bet that efficiency is the moat. Both could be right.

## What This Means Locally

Palm Springs Coachella sits at the intersection of several trends. We're a climate refuge — hot, but with water infrastructure and elevation diversity. We're a remote work destination — three hours from Los Angeles but functionally independent. We're a retirement community, a vacation community, and increasingly a founder community. The valley has always attracted people building things that don't fit elsewhere.

If AI costs keep dropping, more of those things become viable. Custom software for small businesses. Automated services for hospitality. Healthcare tools for an aging population. Education platforms for a distributed workforce. The valley doesn't have a major university or a venture capital ecosystem, which used to be a disadvantage. In an AI-native economy, it might not be. You don't need Stanford if you have Claude and Cursor. You don't need Sequoia if you can build profitably from month one.

This is the bet SunshineFM is making: that intelligence infrastructure matters more than proximity to traditional power centers. That a well-informed community can move faster than a poorly-informed one. That the desert has always been a place where people come to build new things, and the next wave of new things is just starting.
