OpenAI called code red in December. Now we know what that looks like on the ground: top researchers walking out the door, research teams getting their budgets slashed, and a company-wide pivot from science lab to product factory.

Jerry Turek, a seven-year veteran and VP of research, left with a memo saying he's pursuing research that's now "hard to do at OpenAI." Economist Tom Cunningham quit in September with a scorching goodbye note about being tired of serving as an "informal promotional entity" instead of doing rigorous work. The teams behind Sora and DALL-E are reporting funding cuts or denials. If it doesn't make ChatGPT smarter right now, OpenAI doesn't want to pay for it.

This isn't just internal drama. OpenAI is preparing to go public later this year at a valuation likely approaching a trillion dollars. That means the AGI moonshot — the decade-long research project to build AI smarter than every human in every domain — is taking a backseat to monetization. Sam Altman has to ship products people will pay for, and he has to do it fast.

Why? Because Anthropic is eating OpenAI's lunch.

## Claude Code Is Printing a Billion Dollars

Anthropic isn't the scrappy underdog anymore. They're aiming for \$26 billion in revenue this year. Their coding tool, Claude Code, is generating about \$1 billion in revenue with an estimated 3 to 4 million daily active users on Mac alone. OpenAI just released their Mac-native Codex app and hit 200,000 downloads in 24 hours — which sounds impressive until you realize it's a small spec in Claude's rearview mirror.

And there's more. Developers spotted error logs on Google's Vertex AI platform referencing Claude Sonnet 5 as of February 3, 2026. If Claude 5 is imminent, OpenAI might be terrified of losing the smartest model crown entirely.

For those of us building in the Coachella Valley, this product war is good news. Competition means better tools, lower prices, and faster innovation. It also means we're going to keep doing what we're already doing — using multiple models for everything. ChatGPT for one thing, Claude for another, Google Gemini for a third. No single model does it all, and that's not changing anytime soon.

Google's coding tool, Anti Gravity, launched last November and likely has around 800,000 users by now. Cursor hit 1 million users recently — a massive milestone for a startup being squeezed by giants. Replit has 22 million users globally, mostly students and beginners. OpenAI is coming for the pros who use Cursor and Claude, and that's where the real competition lies.

Sam Altman says the dopamine hit from using Codex comes from the fact that AI never gets tired. He likened it to hiring a team of junior developers who all work in parallel on different parts of your project. The models don't run out of dopamine. They don't get tired. They just keep coding.

## The Real Estate Agent Who Can See the Future

A Los Angeles-based startup just raised \$10 million to build what they're calling an operating system for real estate agents. And what they're doing is so obvious in hindsight that you'll wonder why nobody thought of it before.

Right now, if you ask a real estate agent in Palm Springs whether you can build a casita or expand the master suite on a property you're considering, their answer is usually: "Let me find out." They call the city, get a surveyor, talk to someone on their team. If you have a great relationship with your agent, that's fine. But it takes time.

This startup has a feature called Underbuilt. It instantly scans zoning laws, setbacks, and other determining factors, and shows you the property's hidden potential on the spot. It doesn't just show you the house. It shows you the future of the house.

Imagine you're looking for a lot in Movie Colony or up Highway 74 in Palm Desert. Your agent can instantly show you only properties where an ADU fits legally — not based on intuition, but verified by AI intelligence. For renovations, the tool can verify if you can legally expand the footprint before you even make an offer. For land values, it helps justify higher prices for tear-downs by proving the buildable square footage immediately.

This helps agents move from just selling homes to becoming investment advisors. In a market like ours, where inventory is tight and prices are flat, if you can show a client hidden value, you win. The agents who use this will look like geniuses. The ones who don't will look like they're just guessing.

## Starbucks Doesn't Need You to Scoop Ice Anymore

Starbucks is deploying AI voice assistants in the drive-thru. The system filters out noise, ignores the kids yelling in the back seat, and handles the order. The human at the window just hands you your coffee.

They've also installed the Siren system — a bartender robot that dispenses ice and milk and blends drinks automatically. It cuts the time to make a frappuccino from 90 seconds down to 30 seconds. That's a massive labor reduction. You don't need a person scooping ice anymore.

In the back of the house, they're using AI scanning tools to count cups and coffee beans. No more manager with a clipboard spending two hours doing inventory on a Sunday night. The machine does it.

Starbucks CEO Brian Niccol says this is all about removing friction so baristas can connect with customers. But if you've been to a Starbucks lately, you know that's not what's happening. The name on your cup is a sticker, not handwriting. The interaction is transactional.

I love this as a consumer. If I were a manager, I'd love it too. But if I'm a frontline employee, I'm learning to work with digital co-workers — and unless demand goes up in the valley, there's going to be less and less need for a workforce. This is where the trend is heading. I'm not judging it. I'm just saying it's happening.

## Microsoft Copilot: 3.3% Adoption

Microsoft has 450 million commercial seats. Out of that, 3.3% are paying for Copilot. Let that sink in. 3% of Microsoft's massive user base is actually using the AI tool they've been pushing since day one.

I've played around with Copilot. They have a lot of work to do. But 3.3%? That's either a sales miracle or a sign that the product isn't landing the way Microsoft hoped.

## OpenClaw and Moltbook: The Security Nightmare

OpenClaw is the open-source, locally hosted AI agent that takes over your computer and runs your life. A week after it launched, someone built Moltbook — a social network for AI agents. It went from 1,000 agents to 1.5 million agents in a matter of days.

Then the security reports came out.

One single agent spun up half a million accounts. A user named hightower6eu uploaded over 300 fake crypto trading skills to the OpenClaw library. Download one thinking you're getting rich, and your entire machine belongs to them.

Right now, the only thing these agents are effectively automating is your own identity theft. If you're running this stuff at home, disconnect it from your primary machine. Treat it like radioactive material.

OpenClaw is here to stay. Moltbook might be a cautionary tale. But make no mistake — social networks for AI agents with autonomy are coming. Just not like this.
