We're late. Again.

That's the blunt assessment from someone who's spent the last two years trying to get Coachella Valley cities and business leaders to understand what's coming with AI. Not coming — here. Already deployed. Already changing how media gets made, how businesses operate, how people expect to interact with information. And the desert, characteristically, is still catching up to movements that went mainstream elsewhere months ago.

This matters because we're not talking about a nice-to-have technology upgrade. We're talking about the difference between businesses that will exist in three years and businesses that won't. Between cities that can deliver modern services and cities that feel like they're running on dial-up.

## The Acceptance Shift

Something fundamental changed in the last 24 months. Two years ago, if you heard AI-generated audio, you knew it. The tells were obvious. Now? The line's gone blurry, and more importantly, nobody under 30 particularly cares. There's a generational acceptance forming around AI entertainment, AI influencers, AI-assisted everything. Not resistance — acceptance.

That's the context for what Sunshine FM is: a radio station built by a human and AI working together, transparently, in real time. Station IDs created on the fly. Content shaped by what matters locally. No pretense that this is 1970s broadcasting. It's 2026 broadcasting, which means the tools are different and the speed is different and the old gatekeepers don't matter anymore.

The question isn't whether AI belongs in media. It's already there. The question is whether Coachella Valley businesses understand what that means for them.

## Anthropic Just Changed Desktop Computing

While the valley's been sleeping, Anthropic dropped something significant: Claude can now control your computer. Not in some limited, pre-programmed way. Actually control it. Move the mouse. Click buttons. Fill out forms. Navigate between applications.

The technical term is "computer use capability." The practical term is: your AI can now do the tedious work you've been doing manually for years.

This isn't vaporware. It's shipping. And the implications for small business operations in the desert are enormous. Think about the local business owner manually updating inventory across three systems, copying data from emails into spreadsheets, clicking through the same seven-step process fifty times a week. That work just became automatable by something that costs \$20 a month.

The gap between businesses that adopt this and businesses that don't will be visible within quarters, not years. One will have staff focused on growth and customer relationships. The other will have staff doing data entry.

## The Entrepreneurship Paradox

Here's the tension: AI makes starting a business easier than ever. Lower barriers. Faster execution. Less capital required upfront. You can build a functional website, generate marketing content, handle customer service inquiries, manage basic operations — all with AI assistance that would've required hiring multiple people five years ago.

But — and this matters — the *number* of new businesses being created is actually declining. Entrepreneurship rates are down. Despite the tools being better, fewer people are starting companies.

Why? Probably because the tools being better means the competition is fiercer. The bar for "good enough" keeps rising. And the psychological weight of starting something in an economy that feels uncertain is real, regardless of what the technology enables.

For Coachella Valley, this is the moment. We have lower costs of living than coastal California. We have space. We have a growing population. We should be seeing an entrepreneurial boom as people realize they can build here with AI leverage and compete globally without Bay Area overhead.

But only if people know the tools exist. Only if they understand what's possible now that wasn't possible 18 months ago.

## What Perplexity's Revenue Means

Perplexity AI hit \$100 million in annual recurring revenue. For context, that's a search engine alternative that didn't exist in any meaningful commercial form three years ago. It's now processing 20 million queries daily and pulling in nine figures annually.

This is the speed we're operating at. A company goes from zero to \$100 million ARR in the time it used to take to get through Series A fundraising. And it's doing it by fundamentally changing how people find information — not by indexing the web like Google, but by understanding queries and synthesizing answers.

For local businesses, the lesson is clear: people's expectations about how they get information have already changed. If your business model depends on people finding you through traditional search, through directories, through the old discovery mechanisms — you're building on sand.

The new expectation is: I ask a question, I get an answer, and that answer might include your business or it might not, depending on whether the AI thinks you're relevant. SEO is becoming "is your business information structured in a way that AI can understand and recommend you?"

Most Coachella Valley businesses aren't even thinking about this yet.

## The Local Lag Problem

This is the part that's frustrating. The valley is consistently late to tech adoption. Not a little late — years late. We saw it with e-commerce, with social media marketing, with mobile-first design. By the time local businesses adopt something, the early movers elsewhere have already extracted most of the advantage.

AI is moving faster than any of those previous waves. The window where being an early adopter in the desert gives you a meaningful edge is measured in months, not years.

And yet: silence. Most local business conversations are still about traditional marketing, traditional operations, traditional everything. As if the world hasn't fundamentally shifted in the last 24 months.

## What This Means for Building Here

If you're building a business in Palm Springs Coachella right now, you have an unusual opportunity. The infrastructure is improving. The cost basis is favorable compared to coastal markets. The quality of life is high. And the competitive landscape is still sleepy enough that adopting modern AI tools gives you an immediate operational advantage.

But that window is temporary. Once the broader business community here wakes up to what's possible — and they will, eventually — the advantage disappears.

The businesses that will dominate the next five years in this valley are the ones being built right now by people who understand that AI isn't a future consideration. It's the current operating environment. It's how you compete with larger companies despite having a smaller team. It's how you deliver service quality that used to require 10x the headcount.

The question isn't whether AI will reshape Coachella Valley business. It's whether you'll be part of the first wave or the scrambling-to-catch-up wave.

Based on history, most will be in the second group. But you don't have to be.
