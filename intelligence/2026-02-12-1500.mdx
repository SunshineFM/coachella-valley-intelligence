**Anthropic Hits 20% Business Adoption as OpenAI Slips**
Anthropic's business adoption surged from 16.7% to 19.5% in a single month while OpenAI dropped from 36.8% to 35.9%, according to RAMP's AI Index released this week. Claude Cowork — the autonomous task executor that handles spreadsheets, files, and reports without human oversight — is driving much of the growth, and it's the same tool that wiped nearly a trillion dollars off SaaS market caps last week when Microsoft integrations dropped. For Valley businesses still treating AI as "just another tool," this is your wake-up call: the models aren't competing for features anymore, they're competing for your entire workflow.

**Local Performer Cuts \$5K Freelance Writer, Uses ChatGPT Instead**
At a Palm Desert event last night, a local live performance producer told a room of 20 that he replaced his regular freelance writer — who charged \$5,000 for two articles in his quarterly magazine — with ChatGPT after being "not too happy" with her work. He still gets emails praising the AI-written pieces, and he's thrilled with the creativity and cost savings. When asked if AI would replace jobs in the Coachella Valley, he said no — but couldn't reconcile that his own freelancer just lost five grand this cycle. This is the messy middle: individuals making rational decisions that add up to structural chaos, and nobody in the room wanted to do the math on what happens when half the people at that table do the same thing.

**Microsoft Warns 80% of Fortune 500 Face Shadow AI Security Risks**
Only 47% of Fortune 500 companies have implemented security measures specifically for generative AI, even as employees use ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude on personal devices to complete work tasks, according to a Microsoft survey of 1,700 security professionals. Shadow AI — unsanctioned AI use on personal phones and laptops — is now the norm, not the exception, and it's creating massive vulnerabilities when proprietary documents and customer data get uploaded to public models. For Valley mid-market companies (the 200- to 1,000-employee hospitality, healthcare, and real estate firms), this isn't a future problem — your people are already doing this, and you don't have a sandbox or security standard in place yet.

**Waymo to Double Weekly Rides to 1 Million, Expand to 20+ Cities**
Waymo's sixth-generation autonomous vehicles will launch across more than 20 US cities and expand internationally to London and Tokyo this year, with a goal of exceeding 1 million paid weekly rides by year-end — up from 450,000 weekly trips now. The company just raised \$16 billion at a \$126 billion valuation (double what it was a year ago) and secured a deal with Hyundai for 50,000 Ioniq 5 EVs by 2028. This matters for the Valley because AI isn't just chatbots — it's robotaxis, it's ambient sensors in homes, it's the infrastructure layer that's being built while we debate whether prompts are "real work."

**Talkiatree Raises \$210M to Scale AI-Backed Telepsychiatry**
Talkiatree, one of the nation's largest in-network psychiatric care providers, raised \$210 million in Series D funding to scale its model of employing psychiatrists as full-time W2 employees while using AI to support continuity of care. The company surpassed 2 million patient visits last year, doubling its volume, and treats depression, anxiety, ADHD, bipolar disorder, PTSD, and OCD through 60+ health plan networks. This is the attachment economy in action: AI isn't just vying for your attention, it's vying for your mental health relationship, and it's doing so with human psychiatrists in the loop — for now.

**Google DeepMind CEO Predicts AI Golden Era Within 10–15 Years**
Demis Hassabis, Nobel Prize-winning CEO of Google DeepMind, predicted this week that AI will usher in a "new golden era of discovery" by 2036–2040, curing major diseases, solving the energy crisis, and enabling humanity to venture beyond Earth. The vision is radical abundance powered by AI-accelerated science — and there's no reason to doubt him. But here's the timing problem: between now and 2040, you've got 5 to 10 years of chaos, the "messy middle" where humans run companies, governments, and policies that don't agree on how to regulate or deploy AI safely. The techno-optimists are fixated on the future; the rest of us are trying to figure out how to pay rent and provide for families while the transition unfolds.

**Wisdom Raises Pre-Seed to Help Seniors Age in Place with AI Sensors**
Wisdom, a pre-seed AI startup backed by the Richard King Mellon Foundation, is developing sensor-based smart home technology that helps older adults age in place without cameras or wearables. The system uses ambient sensors and edge computing to detect falls and monitor activity patterns, processing data locally on a home hub to address privacy concerns. This matters for the Coachella Valley because we have a massive aging population, healthcare is one of our biggest industries, and by 2030 California alone will need 3 million caregivers — a shortage driven by low wages, 50% turnover, and physical demands. If Wisdom can crack this, it's not just a tech win, it's a lifeline for Valley families trying to keep parents independent.
