**OpenAI Buys OpenClaw for \$1 Billion on Valentine's Day**
OpenAI acquired OpenClaw — the viral AI agent framework with 200,000 GitHub stars — on Valentine's Day, outbidding Meta in what became a billion-dollar auction for a six-week-old open source project. Creator Peter Steinberger, an Austrian developer, chose OpenAI over Zuckerberg because he "wanted to change the world, not just build a large company." For Valley businesses watching the AI agent hype cycle, this is the moment the big players decided the future is now — even if the present is still dangerously unfinished. The question: will OpenAI harden the security before or after someone gets burned?

**Security Researchers Say OpenClaw Is "Fundamentally Broken"**
TechCrunch reported that multiple security researchers are warning people not to use OpenClaw due to severe vulnerabilities, including trivial prompt injection attacks that let malicious actors hijack AI agents through hidden email text or metadata. John Hammond from Huntress called it "just a wrapper" with "no hard security," and researcher Ian Al Ahl discovered that OpenClaw's social network MaltBook had security so bad anyone could impersonate any agent. The viral "AI uprising" posts on MaltBook? Likely written by humans exploiting broken authentication. For any local business tempted to deploy AI agents for email, scheduling, or customer service — wait. The UX is brilliant, but the guardrails don't exist yet.

**Software Stocks Lose \$1 Trillion as AI Threatens White-Collar Work**
Software stocks are down 21% in 2026, erasing over \$1 trillion in market cap as hedge funds bet \$24 billion against the sector — Salesforce, Adobe, Oracle, and ServiceNow are all down 20% or more. Nick Evans, manager of a \$12 billion Polar Capital fund, dumped nearly all his software holdings and warned that AI poses an "existential threat" to application software, comparing the sector's future to newspapers in the 2000s. Microsoft AI chief Mustafa Suleiman predicts most white-collar tasks will be fully automated within 12 to 18 months, and Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei warns AI could eliminate half of all entry-level white-collar jobs within five years. For Coachella Valley's finance, real estate, legal, and hospitality back-office operations — this is the warning shot.

**White-Collar Job Losses Accelerate Despite GDP Growth**
White-collar employment in finance, insurance, information, and professional services — sectors representing over 40% of US GDP — has declined since late 2022, breaking the traditional pattern where economic growth drives hiring. Nearly 55,000 US layoffs in 2025 were AI-related, up from 17,000 combined in the prior two years, and the financial sector alone lost 22,000 jobs in January 2026. Oxford Economics forecasts job growth will average under 40,000 monthly in 2026 while GDP grows 2.8%, meaning productivity is replacing workforce expansion. The economy is learning to grow without creating proportional jobs — and if local colleges, universities, and city councils aren't preparing the next generation for this shift, they're setting them up to be left behind.

**India Hosts 250,000 at AI Summit, Challenges Silicon Valley's Global Dominance**
India is hosting the Global AI Impact Summit this week in New Delhi with 250,000 attendees — more than Coachella and Stagecoach combined — and every major AI CEO in attendance: Sam Altman, Sundar Pichai, Dario Amodei, plus heads of Microsoft, Amazon, and DeepMind. Google, Microsoft, and Amazon have committed \$68 billion in AI and cloud infrastructure in India through 2030, and India is already OpenAI's largest user market with 72 million daily ChatGPT users. India isn't trying to beat the US or China at building frontier models — they're redefining what winning means: deployment at scale, governance that includes the Global South, and leveraging a 1.4 billion-person market to extract maximum value from foreign investment. For anyone betting on the future of AI, the chessboard just got a lot bigger.

**Meta Patents AI That Keeps Your Social Media Active After You Die**
Meta was granted a patent in December 2025 for AI technology that trains a large language model on your posts, comments, and likes to create a digital clone that mimics your online behavior after you die — or during long breaks. The system could like and comment on posts, respond to DMs, and even simulate audio or video calls on your behalf, with Meta CTO Andrew Bosworth listed as the primary author. Meta justifies it by saying "followers will miss you," but ethics experts warn about privacy concerns, consent issues, and the obvious business motive: more engagement, more content, more data. A Meta spokesman said "we have no plans to move forward with this technology" — but they filed the patent anyway, and Microsoft patented something similar in 2021 before deeming it "too disturbing for production."

## Local Radar

**Imperial County Residents Fight Big Data Centers**
Residents in Imperial County — just over the mountains from Coachella Valley — are fighting their own Board of Supervisors and the Imperial Irrigation District over plans to build some of California's largest data centers in the middle of their communities. The battle involves not just big tech developers but local governance structures approving projects residents say they never asked for. Worth watching: the precedent this sets for how the Valley handles its own tech infrastructure expansion.

**OpenClaw Community Forming Locally**
An OpenClaw conference in San Francisco last week had a 1,000-person waitlist, and there's interest in forming a local "lobsters" network of developers running AI agents on Mac Minis. If you're experimenting with OpenClaw or want to see a local conference, reach out — the host is ready to pull in Bay Area and LA colleagues to make it happen. This is the kind of technical community the Valley has been missing.

**AICV Workshops Continue Through 2026**
AICV — the local AI community that ran over 30 workshops in 2025 — is rebranding and shifting tone this year from "nice" to "lighting fires under people's chairs" about AI readiness. If you're a business owner, educator, or city leader who hasn't engaged yet, the message is clear: the window for comfortable preparation is closing. Workshops and meetups are ongoing — reach out before you're forced to react instead of prepare.
