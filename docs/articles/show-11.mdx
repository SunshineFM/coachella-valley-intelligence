## The 30-Year Itch and the Army of Agents

I’ve spent three decades in the trenches of tech and the record industry, from Hollywood hallways to the overnight slots at Claremont Men’s College. I’ve seen every "next big thing" come and go, usually accompanied by an expensive PR firm and a lot of vaporware. But this? This feels different. Sunshine FM is modern media for Palm Springs Coachella, but it’s not built on the old bones of legacy broadcasting. It’s built on the backs of an army of AI agents. I’m one human sitting here, augmented by digital personas that handle everything from production to research. It’s a dream I’ve had since my days at K-Wave in San Clemente, but until now, it required a budget that would make a desert real estate developer blush. Now, it just requires a bit of curiosity and a willingness to break things in real time. We’re building this in public because the "polished" version usually stinks of corporate committee thinking, and frankly, we don’t have time for that.

## Distinguishing Signal from the Silicon Slop

Let’s talk about the elephant in the room: AI Slop. You’ve seen it—those generic, soulless LinkedIn posts and the endless stream of "top 10 prompts" that offer zero actual value. Most of what passes for AI content right now is a race to the bottom of the engagement barrel. Sunshine FM is an experiment in the opposite. We’re trying to find the signal in the noise. It isn't just about using AI to make things; it's about using it to amplify a specific, human taste. I’m not here to be a dj in the traditional sense; I’m a founder trying to see if we can move the needle for the Coachella Valley. The promise is leverage, but the peril is becoming another source of digital noise. We’re leaning into the weird, the unrefined, and the serious. If a tech professional in Rancho Mirage or a small business owner in Indio is going to give us their time, we owe them more than a generated script. We owe them perspective.

## Gravity and the Desert Ecosystem

Why does this matter for us, specifically? Because the Coachella Valley has a habit of sauntering when the rest of the world is sprinting. We have a unique gravity here, but we lack the technical mass to keep the next generation of builders from fleeing to Austin or Miami. By building this intelligence layer, we’re making our home legible to the builders. I’ve got friends in the Bay Area who are about to become very wealthy from the foundational AI boom. They’re going to be looking for their next chapter, and I want to make sure they see the desert as more than just a place for a mid-century weekend. I want them to see a nascent startup ecosystem that understands orchestration over specialization. We aren't just creating a radio station; we are building a lighthouse for the intelligence economy.
